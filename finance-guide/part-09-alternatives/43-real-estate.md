# Chapter 43: Real Estate Investing

## Overview

Real estate has built more wealth for average Americans than almost any other asset class. It offers income, appreciation, tax advantages, and inflation protection. This chapter explores the various ways to invest in real estate—from owning rental properties to buying REITs—and helps you determine what approach might fit your situation.

---

## Why Invest in Real Estate?

### The Appeal

**Income:** Monthly rent provides cash flow
**Appreciation:** Property values tend to rise over time
**Leverage:** Banks lend 80%+ of purchase price
**Tax benefits:** Depreciation, 1031 exchanges, deductions
**Inflation hedge:** Rents and values rise with inflation
**Tangible asset:** Physical property you can see and touch

### Historical Returns

**Long-term real estate appreciation:**
- National average: 3-4% annually (price only)
- With rent income: 8-12% total returns
- Leveraged returns can be much higher

**Compared to stocks:**
- Lower volatility in good times
- Less liquid
- More effort to manage
- Different risk/return profile

## Direct Ownership

### Rental Properties

**The concept:** Buy property, rent it out, build equity while tenants pay mortgage.

### Single-Family Rentals (SFRs)

**Pros:**
- Easier to understand
- Easier to finance
- Easier to sell
- Tenants pay utilities
- Appreciation potential

**Cons:**
- Vacancy = 0% income
- One property = concentrated risk
- Maintenance responsibilities
- Tenant management

### Multi-Family Properties (2-4 Units)

**Pros:**
- Multiple income streams
- Vacancy less devastating
- House hack opportunity (live in one unit)
- Still qualifies for residential financing

**Cons:**
- Higher purchase price
- More complex management
- Common area maintenance
- More turnover generally

### The Numbers That Matter

**Key metrics:**
- **Cap rate:** Net Operating Income / Property Value
- **Cash-on-cash return:** Annual Cash Flow / Cash Invested
- **Gross rent multiplier:** Property Price / Annual Rent

**Example analysis:**

| Metric | Calculation | Value |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Purchase price | | $300,000 |
| Down payment (25%) | | $75,000 |
| Monthly rent | | $2,000 |
| Annual gross rent | $2,000 × 12 | $24,000 |
| Expenses (50% rule) | $24,000 × 50% | $12,000 |
| Net Operating Income | $24,000 - $12,000 | $12,000 |
| Cap rate | $12,000 / $300,000 | 4% |
| Mortgage payment | ~$1,350/month | $16,200/year |
| Annual cash flow | $12,000 - $16,200 | -$4,200 |

**This property is cash flow negative** - Common in high-priced markets. Betting on appreciation.

### The 1% Rule (Quick Screen)

**Rule:** Monthly rent should be at least 1% of purchase price.

**Example:**
- $200,000 property should rent for $2,000/month
- Below 1%: Likely cash flow negative
- Above 1%: Likely cash flow positive

**Reality:** Hard to find in most major markets today.

## Financing Real Estate

### Conventional Mortgages

**Owner-occupied:**
- 3-20% down payment
- Lower interest rates
- Must live there (at least initially)

**Investment property:**
- 20-25% down typically
- Higher interest rates (+0.5-1%)
- Stricter qualification

### Leverage Power

**Why leverage matters:**

**Scenario:** $100,000 investment

| Approach | Investment | Property Value | Appreciation (5%) | Return |
|----------|------------|----------------|-------------------|--------|
| Cash purchase | $100,000 | $100,000 | $5,000 | 5% |
| 25% down | $100,000 | $400,000 | $20,000 | 20% |

**Leverage multiplies returns** - But also amplifies losses.

### Financing Options

**Conventional:** Banks, credit unions
**FHA loans:** Lower down payment, owner-occupied
**VA loans:** Zero down for veterans
**Portfolio loans:** Non-conforming situations
**Hard money:** Short-term, high cost, quick closing
**DSCR loans:** Based on property cash flow, not personal income

## House Hacking

### The Strategy

**Buy a property, live in part, rent out the rest.**

### Common Approaches

**Multi-family:**
- Buy 2-4 unit building
- Live in one unit
- Rent others
- They pay your mortgage

**Single-family with ADU:**
- Buy house with accessory dwelling unit
- Live in main house or ADU
- Rent the other

**Room rental:**
- Buy single-family home
- Rent out rooms
- Lowest barrier to entry

### Benefits

- Owner-occupied financing (lower down payment, better rates)
- Build investing experience while living there
- Drastically reduce or eliminate housing cost
- Learn property management hands-on

## Tax Advantages

### Depreciation

**What it is:** IRS allows you to deduct the "wear and tear" on rental property.

**How it works:**
- Residential property: 27.5-year depreciation schedule
- $275,000 building value / 27.5 = $10,000/year deduction
- Reduces taxable income (not cash flow)

**Example:**
- Rental income: $24,000
- Expenses: $12,000
- Depreciation: $10,000
- Taxable income: $2,000 (instead of $12,000)

### Mortgage Interest Deduction

**Interest on rental property loans is fully deductible** against rental income.

### Other Deductions

- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Repairs and maintenance
- Property management fees
- Travel to manage properties
- Professional fees (accountant, attorney)

### 1031 Exchange

**What it is:** Defer capital gains taxes by exchanging one investment property for another.

**Requirements:**
- Investment or business property (not primary residence)
- Identify replacement property within 45 days
- Close within 180 days
- Same or greater value

**Power:** Never pay capital gains if you keep exchanging until death (stepped-up basis).

## Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

### What Are REITs?

**REITs are companies that own, operate, or finance income-producing real estate** and trade on stock exchanges.

### How REITs Work

**Requirements to qualify:**
- Invest 75%+ of assets in real estate
- Derive 75%+ of income from real estate
- Distribute 90%+ of taxable income as dividends
- Have 100+ shareholders

### Types of REITs

**Equity REITs (most common):**
- Own and operate properties
- Collect rent
- Examples: Office buildings, apartments, malls

**Mortgage REITs (mREITs):**
- Invest in mortgages/mortgage-backed securities
- Earn interest income
- More volatile, interest rate sensitive

**Hybrid REITs:**
- Combination of equity and mortgage

### REIT Sectors

| Sector | Examples | Characteristics |
|--------|----------|-----------------|
| Residential | Apartment complexes | Recession-resistant |
| Retail | Malls, shopping centers | E-commerce pressured |
| Office | Corporate buildings | Remote work concerns |
| Industrial | Warehouses, logistics | E-commerce beneficiary |
| Healthcare | Hospitals, senior living | Demographics tailwind |
| Data Centers | Server facilities | Digital growth play |
| Cell Towers | Telecom infrastructure | 5G growth |
| Self-Storage | Storage facilities | Recession-resistant |

### REIT Advantages

**Liquidity:** Buy and sell like stocks
**Diversification:** Own pieces of many properties
**Professional management:** No landlord duties
**Low minimum:** Buy one share
**Income:** High dividend yields (typically 3-6%+)

### REIT Disadvantages

**Correlation:** More correlated with stocks than direct real estate
**Taxes:** Dividends taxed as ordinary income (not qualified)
**No leverage benefit:** Can't use bank financing
**No control:** Management makes all decisions

### Popular REIT ETFs

| ETF | Focus | Expense Ratio |
|-----|-------|---------------|
| VNQ | Broad US REITs | 0.12% |
| SCHH | US REITs | 0.07% |
| VNQI | International REITs | 0.12% |
| O | Single REIT (Realty Income) | N/A |

## Real Estate Crowdfunding

### What It Is

**Platforms that pool money from investors to fund real estate projects.**

### Major Platforms

**Fundrise:** Diversified portfolios, low minimums
**CrowdStreet:** Individual deals, accredited investors
**RealtyMogul:** Mix of approaches
**Arrived Homes:** Single-family rental shares

### Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Lower minimums than direct ownership
- Diversification across properties
- Passive (no management)
- Access to commercial deals

**Cons:**
- Illiquid (lock-up periods)
- Platform risk
- Limited track record
- Fees can be high

## Getting Started in Real Estate

### Path 1: REITs (Simplest)

1. Open brokerage account
2. Buy REIT ETF (VNQ) or individual REITs
3. Hold in tax-advantaged account if possible
4. Reinvest dividends

### Path 2: House Hacking

1. Save for down payment (3.5-5% FHA)
2. Find 2-4 unit property
3. Get owner-occupied financing
4. Live in one unit, rent others
5. After 1 year, consider repeat

### Path 3: Traditional Rental

1. Save 20-25% down payment + reserves
2. Learn market and property analysis
3. Build team (agent, lender, contractor)
4. Buy first property
5. Manage or hire property manager

## Key Takeaways

- **Real estate builds wealth through income, appreciation, and leverage**
- **Direct ownership offers the most benefits** - But requires the most work and capital
- **REITs provide liquid exposure** - Simple but lacks tax advantages and leverage
- **House hacking is the best start** - Owner-occupied financing, learn while you earn
- **Cash flow is king** - Appreciation is a bonus, not a guarantee
- **Leverage amplifies returns and risks** - Don't over-leverage
- **Tax advantages are significant** - Depreciation, 1031 exchanges, deductions
- **Real estate is not passive** - Even REITs require monitoring and rebalancing
