# Chapter 44: Private Equity & Venture Capital

## Overview

Private equity (PE) and venture capital (VC) invest in companies that aren't publicly traded. These asset classes have generated strong returns for institutional investors and the ultra-wealthy, but they remain largely inaccessible to most individual investors. This chapter explains how they work and the limited ways retail investors can gain exposure.

---

## What Is Private Equity?

### Definition

**Private equity refers to investments in companies that are not listed on public stock exchanges.**

PE firms raise money from investors, acquire or invest in private companies, improve them, and sell for a profit.

### Types of Private Equity

**Leveraged Buyouts (LBOs):**
- Buy established companies using significant debt
- Improve operations, cut costs, grow revenue
- Sell or take public in 3-7 years
- Most common PE strategy

**Growth Equity:**
- Minority investments in growing companies
- Less debt than LBOs
- Help companies scale
- Less control than buyouts

**Distressed/Turnaround:**
- Buy troubled companies cheaply
- Restructure and fix problems
- Sell once healthy
- High risk, high reward

### How PE Works

**The process:**
1. PE firm raises fund (commitments from investors)
2. Identifies and acquires target companies
3. Implements operational improvements
4. Holds for 3-7 years
5. Exits (sells company, IPO, or secondary sale)
6. Returns capital plus profits to investors

### PE Return Structure

**The "2 and 20" model:**
- 2% annual management fee (on committed capital)
- 20% carried interest (share of profits above hurdle rate)

**Example (simple):**
- $100M fund
- Management fee: $2M/year
- Fund doubles to $200M
- Carry: 20% × $100M profit = $20M to PE firm
- Investors: $80M profit

## What Is Venture Capital?

### Definition

**Venture capital is private equity focused on early-stage, high-growth startups.**

VCs provide capital to companies too young or unproven for public markets or traditional lending.

### Stages of VC Investment

| Stage | Typical Investment | Company Status |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|
| Pre-seed/Seed | $100K - $2M | Idea or early product |
| Series A | $2M - $15M | Product-market fit |
| Series B | $15M - $50M | Scaling |
| Series C+ | $50M+ | Expansion, pre-IPO |

### The VC Model

**High failure rate, big winners:**
- Most startups fail
- VCs expect 50-70% of investments to fail
- 1-2 "home runs" drive fund returns
- A 10x or 100x return offsets many failures

**Example portfolio:**
- Invest in 10 companies at $1M each = $10M
- 6 fail completely: $0
- 2 return 2x: $4M
- 1 returns 5x: $5M
- 1 returns 50x: $50M
- Total return: $59M on $10M (5.9x)

### VC vs. PE

| Factor | Venture Capital | Private Equity |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| Company stage | Early/startup | Mature |
| Risk profile | Very high | High |
| Leverage used | Little/none | Significant |
| Expected failures | 50-70% | 10-20% |
| Target returns | 3-5x+ fund | 2-3x fund |
| Holding period | 5-10 years | 3-7 years |

## Who Can Invest?

### Accredited Investor Requirements (US)

**To invest in PE/VC funds, you must be "accredited":**

**Income test:**
- $200,000+ income ($300,000 with spouse) for 2 consecutive years
- Reasonable expectation of same in current year

**Net worth test:**
- $1,000,000+ net worth (excluding primary residence)

**Or professional credentials:**
- Series 7, 65, or 82 licenses

### Why the Restrictions?

**SEC rationale:**
- Private investments are risky
- Less regulatory oversight
- Less disclosure than public companies
- Assumes wealthy/sophisticated investors can handle losses

### Minimum Investments

**Typical PE/VC fund minimums:**
- Top-tier funds: $5-10M minimum
- Mid-tier funds: $250K-1M
- Emerging managers: $100K-250K

**This excludes most retail investors.**

## Ways Retail Investors Can Access PE/VC

### Publicly Traded PE Firms

**Buy stock in PE companies themselves:**
- Blackstone (BX)
- KKR (KKR)
- Apollo Global (APO)
- Carlyle Group (CG)

**Pros:** Easy access, liquidity, dividends
**Cons:** Stock price, not direct fund returns; correlated with markets

### Business Development Companies (BDCs)

**What they are:** Publicly traded companies that lend to and invest in middle-market businesses.

**Examples:**
- Ares Capital (ARCC)
- Main Street Capital (MAIN)
- Owl Rock Capital (ORCC)

**Pros:** High dividends (7-10%+), liquid
**Cons:** Interest rate sensitive, credit risk, complex structures

### Closed-End Funds

**Some closed-end funds invest in private companies:**
- Often trade at discounts to NAV
- Limited options available

### Interval Funds

**Semi-liquid structures offering PE/VC exposure:**
- Quarterly liquidity (limited)
- Lower minimums than traditional PE
- Examples: Various asset managers offer these

### Equity Crowdfunding

**Invest in startups directly through platforms:**
- AngelList, StartEngine, Republic, Wefunder
- Minimums as low as $100
- Non-accredited investors allowed (limits apply)

**Risks:**
- Most startups fail
- Extremely illiquid
- Limited due diligence
- Years to any potential return

## Understanding PE/VC Returns

### The J-Curve

**Early years show negative returns** because:
- Management fees are charged immediately
- Investments take time to appreciate
- Exits happen later in fund life

**Visualization:**
```
Return
    |
    |                    ****
    |               ****
    |          ****
    |     ****
Year|*****________________________
 1  |****
 2  |   ***
 3  |      **
    |_____________________________
```

### IRR vs. Multiple

**IRR (Internal Rate of Return):**
- Annualized return considering timing
- Favors faster returns
- Industry standard metric

**Multiple (TVPI - Total Value to Paid-In):**
- Total value / Amount invested
- Ignores timing
- "We returned 2.5x"

**Example:**
- $100M invested, $250M returned over 10 years
- Multiple: 2.5x
- IRR: ~9.6% annualized

### What's a Good Return?

**PE/VC funds target:**
- Top quartile PE: 15-20%+ net IRR
- Top quartile VC: 20-30%+ net IRR
- Median returns are lower and inconsistent

**Important:** Past returns don't guarantee future performance, and top funds often aren't accepting new investors.

## Risks of PE/VC Investing

### Illiquidity

**Your money is locked up:**
- PE: 7-10 years typical
- VC: 10-12 years possible
- No secondary market (or very limited)
- Capital calls can come at any time

### Lack of Transparency

**Limited information:**
- Quarterly reports, not daily prices
- Valuation is subjective until exit
- Less regulatory oversight
- Managers have significant discretion

### Manager Selection Risk

**Significant performance dispersion:**
- Difference between best and worst managers is huge
- Top quartile vs. bottom quartile can be 20%+ IRR difference
- Access to best managers is limited

### Fees

**Expensive:**
- 2% management fee annually
- 20% carried interest on profits
- Fund expenses on top
- 5-7% drag just from fees

### Concentration

**Less diversification:**
- Typical PE fund: 10-20 companies
- Typical VC fund: 20-30 companies
- Any single failure has meaningful impact

## Should You Invest in PE/VC?

### Arguments For

- Potential for higher returns
- Access to private company growth
- Diversification from public markets
- Forces long-term holding (can be good discipline)

### Arguments Against

- Most investors don't qualify
- Illiquidity is severe
- High fees eat returns
- Access to top managers is limited
- Public markets offer good returns with liquidity
- J-curve means years before any return

### The Honest Assessment

**For most investors:**
- Stick with public markets
- Similar returns available with liquidity
- No minimum investment required
- More transparency and lower fees

**PE/VC make sense if:**
- You have substantial wealth ($5M+)
- Don't need the money for 10+ years
- Have access to top-tier managers
- Understand and accept the risks

## Angel Investing (Alternative Approach)

### What Is Angel Investing?

**Wealthy individuals investing directly in startups**, usually at the earliest stages.

### Requirements

- Accredited investor status
- Typically $25K-100K per investment
- Expect to invest in 20+ companies
- 7-10 year time horizon

### Angel Investing Realities

**Most investments will fail:**
- 50-70% complete loss
- 20-30% return some capital
- 5-10% generate meaningful returns
- 1-2% might be home runs

**This is not investing—it's high-risk speculation** unless you have specific expertise and enough capital to diversify.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **PE/VC invest in private companies** - Potentially higher returns, higher risks
- **Most investors don't qualify** - Accredited investor requirements exclude most
- **Minimums are high** - $100K-10M to access quality funds
- **Illiquidity is severe** - 7-12 year lock-ups common
- **Manager selection is critical** - Huge dispersion between top and bottom
- **Fees are high** - 2 and 20 structure, plus expenses
- **Public market alternatives exist** - PE firm stocks, BDCs offer exposure
- **For most people, stick with public markets** - Simpler, liquid, lower cost
