# Chapter 46: Other Alternative Investments

## Overview

Beyond stocks, bonds, real estate, and the alternatives we've covered, there's a universe of other investment options. This chapter explores collectibles, precious metals, structured products, and other alternatives. For most investors, these are optional—but understanding them helps you make informed decisions when they're presented.

---

## Precious Metals (Beyond Gold)

### Silver

**Characteristics:**
- More volatile than gold
- Industrial and investment demand
- Lower price per ounce (more accessible)
- Historically trades at 60-80:1 ratio to gold

**Investment options:**
- Physical (coins, bars)
- ETFs (SLV)
- Mining stocks

**Considerations:**
- Higher volatility than gold
- Industrial demand ties to economy
- Heavier/bulkier to store physical

### Platinum and Palladium

**Characteristics:**
- Primarily industrial metals (catalytic converters)
- Smaller markets, more volatile
- Supply concentrated in few countries

**Considerations:**
- More speculative than gold/silver
- Tied to auto industry demand
- Not traditional "safe haven"

### Precious Metals Summary

| Metal | Primary Use | Volatility | Safe Haven |
|-------|-------------|------------|------------|
| Gold | Investment/Jewelry | Moderate | Yes |
| Silver | Industrial/Investment | High | Somewhat |
| Platinum | Industrial | High | No |
| Palladium | Industrial | Very High | No |

## Collectibles

### Art

**Potential benefits:**
- Appreciation over time
- Aesthetic enjoyment
- Status/prestige

**Challenges:**
- Illiquid (takes time to sell)
- Authentication concerns
- Storage and insurance costs
- No income generation
- Highly subjective value
- Capital gains taxed at 28%

**Who should consider:** Those with genuine interest, expertise, and significant wealth.

### Wine

**Investment-grade wine:**
- Fine Bordeaux, Burgundy
- Requires proper storage
- Authentication critical
- Specialized knowledge required

**Challenges:**
- Storage costs (temperature-controlled)
- Insurance
- Counterfeits prevalent
- Illiquid market

### Classic Cars

**Potential:**
- Significant appreciation for rare models
- Enjoyment value
- Tangible asset

**Challenges:**
- Maintenance costs
- Storage requirements
- Insurance
- Market knowledge required
- Illiquid

### Sports Memorabilia and Trading Cards

**Recent growth:**
- Significant appreciation in rare items
- Grading services provide authentication
- Online platforms improve liquidity

**Challenges:**
- Speculative
- Condition-sensitive
- Fraud concerns
- Trends change

### Collectibles Reality Check

**For most investors, collectibles are not investments—they're hobbies that sometimes appreciate.**

**Problems:**
- No income
- Storage/insurance costs
- Illiquid
- Taxed at 28% (not long-term capital gains rate)
- Subjective valuation
- Requires expertise

**Better approach:** Buy what you love and would keep anyway. Any appreciation is a bonus.

## Structured Products

### What Are Structured Products?

**Pre-packaged investments combining derivatives and traditional assets** to create specific risk/return profiles.

### Common Types

**Principal-Protected Notes:**
- Promise to return principal at maturity
- Upside linked to index or asset
- Limited participation in gains

**Buffered/Barrier Notes:**
- Some downside protection (e.g., first 10% of loss protected)
- Capped upside
- Complex payoff structures

**Reverse Convertibles:**
- High coupon payments
- Principal at risk if underlying falls below barrier
- Essentially selling put options

### Example: Buffered Note

**Structure:**
- 2-year note on S&P 500
- 10% downside buffer
- 15% upside cap

**Outcomes:**
- S&P up 20%: You get 15% (capped)
- S&P up 10%: You get 10%
- S&P down 5%: You get principal back (buffered)
- S&P down 15%: You lose 5% (first 10% buffered)
- S&P down 30%: You lose 20% (first 10% buffered)

### Structured Product Concerns

**Problems:**
- Complex (hard to evaluate)
- High fees (often hidden)
- Issuer credit risk
- Illiquid (early exit penalties)
- Tax inefficient
- Often worse than simple alternatives

**For most investors:** A combination of index funds and bonds achieves similar results with more transparency and lower cost.

## Annuities

### What Are Annuities?

**Insurance products that provide income**, often for retirement.

### Types

**Immediate annuity:**
- Give lump sum today
- Receive payments immediately
- Simple, transparent

**Deferred annuity:**
- Invest over time
- Begin payments later
- Tax-deferred growth

**Fixed annuity:**
- Guaranteed interest rate
- Principal protected
- Like a CD from an insurance company

**Variable annuity:**
- Invested in sub-accounts (mutual fund-like)
- Returns vary
- Often high fees

**Indexed annuity:**
- Returns linked to index
- Some principal protection
- Complex participation rates and caps

### Annuity Considerations

**Potential benefits:**
- Guaranteed income
- Tax deferral
- Longevity protection (can't outlive payments)

**Concerns:**
- High fees (especially variable)
- Surrender charges (locked up for years)
- Complexity
- Inflation risk (fixed payments)
- Insurance company credit risk

**When annuities might make sense:**
- Income-focused retirees
- Those who've maxed other tax-advantaged accounts
- Want guaranteed floor of income

**When to avoid:**
- High-fee variable annuities in tax-advantaged accounts
- Young investors
- Anyone not fully understanding the product

## Certificates of Deposit (CDs)

### What Are CDs?

**Time deposits at banks** with fixed interest rate and maturity.

### CD Characteristics

- FDIC insured (up to $250,000)
- Fixed interest rate
- Penalty for early withdrawal
- Terms from 3 months to 5+ years

### CD Laddering

**Strategy:** Buy CDs with staggered maturities.

**Example:**
- $20,000 in 1-year CD
- $20,000 in 2-year CD
- $20,000 in 3-year CD
- $20,000 in 4-year CD
- $20,000 in 5-year CD

**When 1-year matures:** Reinvest in new 5-year CD.

### When CDs Make Sense

- Emergency fund overflow
- Known near-term expense
- Extreme risk aversion
- Rate higher than money market

### CDs vs. Treasury Bonds

| Factor | CDs | Treasuries |
|--------|-----|------------|
| Safety | FDIC insured | Full faith of US |
| Liquidity | Early withdrawal penalty | Can sell anytime |
| Tax | Federal and state | Federal only |
| Purchase | Through bank | TreasuryDirect, broker |

## Timber

### Timber as Investment

**Characteristics:**
- Trees grow regardless of economy
- Inflation hedge
- Low correlation to stocks
- Harvesting can be timed
- Carbon credits potential

**Access methods:**
- Timberland REITs (Weyerhaeuser, PotlatchDeltic)
- Timber funds (accredited investors)
- Direct ownership (large capital required)

**Considerations:**
- Long time horizon
- Geographic/weather risk
- Illiquid (except REITs)

## Infrastructure

### What Is Infrastructure Investing?

**Investment in physical systems:** Roads, bridges, airports, utilities, cell towers, pipelines.

**Characteristics:**
- Steady cash flows (often regulated)
- Inflation protection (rate adjustments)
- Long-lived assets
- Essential services

### Access Methods

**Infrastructure ETFs:**
- PAVE (US infrastructure)
- IGF (Global infrastructure)

**Master Limited Partnerships (MLPs):**
- Pipeline companies
- Pass-through taxation
- Complex tax reporting

**Utility stocks:** Overlap with infrastructure theme.

## Farmland

### Farmland Investment

**Characteristics:**
- Produces income (crop yields)
- Land appreciation over time
- Inflation hedge
- Low correlation to stocks

**Access methods:**
- REITs (Farmland Partners - FPI)
- Crowdfunding platforms (AcreTrader, FarmFundr)
- Direct ownership (large capital)

**Considerations:**
- Illiquid (except REITs)
- Weather/climate risk
- Commodity price exposure
- Management required

## What Should Be in Your Portfolio?

### Core Holdings (Essential)

**For most investors:**
- Stocks (domestic and international)
- Bonds (various types based on needs)
- Cash (emergency fund, short-term needs)

### Satellite Holdings (Optional)

**Consider based on circumstances:**
- Real estate (REITs or direct)
- Commodities (small allocation)
- TIPS (inflation protection)

### Alternatives (Proceed with Caution)

**Only if appropriate:**
- Precious metals (5% max)
- Alternatives discussed in this chapter

### Skip Unless Expert

**For most people, avoid:**
- Individual collectibles as investments
- Structured products
- High-fee annuities
- Anything you don't understand

## Alternative Investment Checklist

**Before investing in any alternative, ask:**

- [ ] Do I understand how this investment works?
- [ ] What are all the fees (obvious and hidden)?
- [ ] How liquid is this investment?
- [ ] What is the tax treatment?
- [ ] Does this actually add diversification?
- [ ] Am I being sold something that benefits the seller more than me?
- [ ] Could I achieve similar results with simpler investments?
- [ ] Am I investing or speculating?

## Key Takeaways

- **Precious metals beyond gold are more speculative** - Silver is accessible, others are industrial
- **Collectibles are hobbies, not investments** - Buy what you love, not for returns
- **Structured products are often overcomplicated** - High fees, better alternatives exist
- **Annuities have a place for some retirees** - But avoid high-fee variable annuities
- **CDs are simple and safe** - Useful for short-term, known expenses
- **Farmland and timber offer diversification** - But illiquid and specialized
- **Most alternatives aren't necessary** - A simple portfolio often wins
- **Complexity usually favors the seller** - When in doubt, keep it simple
