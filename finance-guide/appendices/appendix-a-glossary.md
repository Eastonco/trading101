# Appendix A: Glossary of Financial Terms

A comprehensive reference of key terms used throughout this guide.

---

## A

**Accumulation Phase:** The period during which an investor is actively saving and building wealth, typically during working years.

**Alpha:** The excess return of an investment relative to its benchmark. Positive alpha means outperformance.

**Annual Percentage Yield (APY):** The effective annual rate of return considering compound interest.

**Appreciation:** An increase in the value of an asset over time.

**Asset Allocation:** The process of dividing investments among different asset categories (stocks, bonds, cash, etc.).

**Asset Class:** A category of investments with similar characteristics (equities, fixed income, real estate, etc.).

**At-the-Money (ATM):** An option whose strike price equals the current price of the underlying asset.

---

## B

**Backdoor Roth IRA:** A strategy to contribute to a Roth IRA by first contributing to a traditional IRA and then converting.

**Backwardation:** A futures market condition where near-term contracts are priced higher than distant contracts.

**Basis Point:** One hundredth of one percent (0.01%). 100 basis points = 1%.

**Bear Market:** A market decline of 20% or more from recent highs.

**Beneficiary:** A person designated to receive the benefits of an account upon the owner's death.

**Beta:** A measure of a security's volatility relative to the market. Beta of 1 = moves with market.

**Bid-Ask Spread:** The difference between the highest price a buyer will pay and the lowest price a seller will accept.

**Blue Chip:** A large, established, financially sound company with a history of reliable performance.

**Bond:** A debt security representing a loan to a government or corporation that pays interest.

**Book Value:** A company's total assets minus total liabilities; also called net asset value.

**Bull Market:** A sustained period of rising stock prices, typically 20% or more from recent lows.

**Buy and Hold:** An investment strategy of purchasing securities and holding them for long periods regardless of market fluctuations.

---

## C

**Call Option:** A contract giving the buyer the right (not obligation) to buy an asset at a specified price within a specified time.

**Capital Gain:** The profit from selling an asset for more than its purchase price.

**Capital Loss:** The loss from selling an asset for less than its purchase price.

**Capitalization (Market Cap):** The total market value of a company's outstanding shares. Market Cap = Share Price × Shares Outstanding.

**Catch-Up Contribution:** Additional retirement account contributions allowed for individuals age 50 and older.

**Compound Interest:** Interest calculated on both the initial principal and accumulated interest from previous periods.

**Contango:** A futures market condition where distant contracts are priced higher than near-term contracts.

**Correction:** A market decline of 10-20% from recent highs.

**Correlation:** A statistical measure of how two securities move in relation to each other (-1 to +1).

**Cost Basis:** The original value of an asset for tax purposes, typically the purchase price plus associated costs.

**Covered Call:** An options strategy where an investor owns stock and sells call options against it.

**Credit Spread:** An options strategy that receives premium by selling a higher-priced option and buying a lower-priced option.

---

## D

**Day Trading:** Buying and selling securities within the same trading day.

**Debit Spread:** An options strategy that requires paying premium by buying a higher-priced option and selling a lower-priced option.

**Decumulation:** The phase of retirement when assets are being drawn down for income.

**Delta:** An options Greek measuring how much an option's price changes relative to the underlying asset's price change.

**Depreciation:** A decrease in the value of an asset over time.

**Diversification:** Spreading investments across various assets to reduce risk.

**Dividend:** A distribution of a company's earnings to shareholders.

**Dividend Yield:** Annual dividends per share divided by share price, expressed as a percentage.

**Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA):** Investing fixed amounts at regular intervals regardless of price.

**Drawdown:** The peak-to-trough decline in an investment or portfolio.

**Duration:** A measure of a bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes.

---

## E

**Earnings Per Share (EPS):** A company's profit divided by its number of outstanding shares.

**EBITDA:** Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization—a profitability measure.

**Emergency Fund:** Cash reserves set aside for unexpected expenses, typically 3-6 months of living expenses.

**Emerging Markets:** Developing countries with growing economies and capital markets.

**Equity:** Ownership in a company (stock), or the value of ownership in an asset after subtracting debts.

**ESPP (Employee Stock Purchase Plan):** A company program allowing employees to purchase company stock at a discount.

**ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund):** A fund that trades on an exchange like a stock and typically tracks an index.

**Ex-Dividend Date:** The first day a stock trades without the right to receive the next dividend.

**Expense Ratio:** The annual fee charged by a fund as a percentage of assets under management.

**Expiration (Options):** The date when an options contract becomes invalid.

---

## F

**FDIC:** Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, which insures bank deposits up to $250,000.

**Fiduciary:** A person or entity legally obligated to act in another party's best interest.

**Fill:** The execution of an order to buy or sell securities.

**Float:** The number of shares available for public trading.

**Free Cash Flow:** Cash generated by a company after capital expenditures.

**Front-End Load:** A sales charge paid when purchasing mutual fund shares.

**Fundamental Analysis:** Evaluating securities by analyzing financial statements, industry conditions, and economic factors.

**Futures Contract:** An agreement to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price on a specific future date.

---

## G

**Gamma:** An options Greek measuring the rate of change of delta.

**Gap:** A price area on a chart where no trading occurred, visible as a break between price bars.

**Glide Path:** The scheduled change in asset allocation over time, typically becoming more conservative.

**Good-Till-Canceled (GTC):** An order that remains active until executed or explicitly canceled.

**Greeks (Options):** Measures of an option's sensitivity to various factors (delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho).

**Gross Domestic Product (GDP):** The total value of goods and services produced by a country.

**Growth Stock:** A stock expected to grow earnings faster than the market average.

---

## H

**Hedge:** An investment made to reduce the risk of adverse price movements in another investment.

**High-Yield Bond:** A bond rated below investment grade, offering higher yield due to higher default risk. Also called "junk bond."

**HSA (Health Savings Account):** A tax-advantaged account for medical expenses available with high-deductible health plans.

---

## I

**Index:** A measurement of the value of a section of the stock market, like the S&P 500.

**Index Fund:** A mutual fund or ETF designed to track the performance of a specific index.

**Inflation:** The rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises over time.

**Initial Public Offering (IPO):** The first sale of stock by a private company to the public.

**In-the-Money (ITM):** An option with intrinsic value—calls when stock > strike, puts when stock < strike.

**Interest Rate:** The percentage charged for borrowing money or earned on savings.

**Intrinsic Value:** For options, the difference between the stock price and strike price (if favorable). For stocks, the underlying value based on fundamentals.

**IRA (Individual Retirement Account):** A tax-advantaged account for retirement savings.

**Iron Condor:** An options strategy combining a bull put spread and bear call spread.

**IRMAA:** Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount—Medicare premium surcharges for higher-income retirees.

---

## J

**January Effect:** The historical tendency for stocks to perform better in January.

**Junk Bond:** See High-Yield Bond.

---

## K

**Kelly Criterion:** A formula for calculating optimal bet size based on edge and probability.

---

## L

**Large Cap:** Companies with market capitalization over $10 billion.

**LEAPS:** Long-Term Equity Anticipation Securities—options with expiration dates over one year away.

**Leverage:** Using borrowed money to increase potential returns (and risks).

**Limit Order:** An order to buy or sell at a specific price or better.

**Liquidity:** The ease with which an asset can be converted to cash without significant price impact.

**Load (Fund):** A sales charge paid when buying (front-end) or selling (back-end) mutual fund shares.

**Long Position:** Owning an asset with expectation it will rise in value.

---

## M

**Margin:** Borrowed money used to purchase securities.

**Margin Call:** A demand for additional funds when the equity in a margin account falls below requirements.

**Market Capitalization:** See Capitalization.

**Market Maker:** A firm that provides liquidity by continuously quoting buy and sell prices.

**Market Order:** An order to buy or sell immediately at the best available price.

**Maturity (Bond):** The date when a bond's principal is repaid to the investor.

**Mean Reversion:** The theory that prices tend to return to their average over time.

**Mid Cap:** Companies with market capitalization between $2 billion and $10 billion.

**Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT):** A theory for constructing portfolios to maximize expected return for a given level of risk.

**Momentum:** The tendency for assets that have performed well to continue performing well in the near term.

**Monte Carlo Simulation:** A computational technique using random sampling to estimate outcomes.

**Moving Average:** The average price of a security over a specific time period, recalculated as new data becomes available.

**Municipal Bond (Muni):** A bond issued by a state or local government, often with tax-exempt interest.

**Mutual Fund:** A pooled investment vehicle managed by professionals that invests in various securities.

---

## N

**NAV (Net Asset Value):** The per-share value of a mutual fund, calculated daily.

**Net Worth:** Total assets minus total liabilities.

**No-Load Fund:** A mutual fund that doesn't charge sales commissions.

---

## O

**Option:** A contract giving the right (not obligation) to buy or sell an asset at a specified price within a specified time.

**Out-of-the-Money (OTM):** An option with no intrinsic value—calls when stock < strike, puts when stock > strike.

**Overvalued:** A security trading above its perceived intrinsic value.

---

## P

**P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings):** Stock price divided by earnings per share.

**P/B Ratio (Price-to-Book):** Stock price divided by book value per share.

**P/S Ratio (Price-to-Sales):** Market capitalization divided by annual revenue.

**Par Value (Bond):** The face value of a bond, typically $1,000.

**Passive Investing:** An investment strategy that tracks a market index rather than trying to beat it.

**Pattern Day Trader:** A trader who executes 4+ day trades within 5 business days, subject to special rules.

**PEG Ratio:** P/E ratio divided by earnings growth rate.

**Portfolio:** A collection of investments held by an individual or institution.

**Premium (Bond):** A bond trading above its par value.

**Premium (Options):** The price paid for an options contract.

**Put Option:** A contract giving the buyer the right (not obligation) to sell an asset at a specified price.

---

## Q

**Qualified Dividend:** A dividend eligible for lower long-term capital gains tax rates.

**Quantitative Easing (QE):** Central bank policy of purchasing securities to inject money into the economy.

---

## R

**Rally:** A period of sustained increases in prices.

**Rebalancing:** Adjusting portfolio holdings back to target allocations.

**REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust):** A company that owns and operates income-producing real estate.

**Required Minimum Distribution (RMD):** The minimum amount that must be withdrawn from retirement accounts starting at age 73.

**Resistance:** A price level where selling pressure tends to prevent further price increases.

**Return on Equity (ROE):** Net income divided by shareholders' equity.

**Risk-Adjusted Return:** Investment return accounting for the amount of risk taken.

**Risk Capacity:** The financial ability to withstand losses.

**Risk Tolerance:** The emotional ability to handle investment volatility.

**Rollover:** Moving retirement funds from one account to another without tax consequences.

**Roth IRA/401(k):** Retirement accounts funded with after-tax dollars; qualified withdrawals are tax-free.

**Rule of 72:** A formula to estimate years to double an investment: 72 ÷ interest rate = years to double.

---

## S

**S&P 500:** An index of 500 large U.S. companies, widely used as a market benchmark.

**SEC:** Securities and Exchange Commission—the federal agency regulating securities markets.

**Sector:** A group of companies operating in similar business areas.

**Securities:** Financial instruments representing ownership (stocks) or debt (bonds).

**Sequence of Returns Risk:** The risk that the timing of withdrawals and market returns negatively impacts portfolio longevity.

**Sharpe Ratio:** A measure of risk-adjusted return: (Return - Risk-Free Rate) ÷ Standard Deviation.

**Short Position:** Selling borrowed securities with the expectation of buying them back at a lower price.

**Short Squeeze:** Rapid price increase forcing short sellers to buy back shares, further driving up prices.

**SIPC:** Securities Investor Protection Corporation—protects customers of failed brokerage firms up to $500,000.

**Small Cap:** Companies with market capitalization under $2 billion.

**Spread (Options):** An options strategy involving two or more contracts with different strike prices or expiration dates.

**Standard Deviation:** A measure of volatility showing how much returns vary from the average.

**Stock Split:** An increase in shares outstanding with a proportional decrease in share price.

**Stop-Loss Order:** An order to sell a security when it reaches a specified price.

**Strike Price:** The price at which an option can be exercised.

**Support:** A price level where buying pressure tends to prevent further price decreases.

---

## T

**Target-Date Fund:** A mutual fund that automatically adjusts asset allocation based on a target retirement year.

**Tax-Loss Harvesting:** Selling investments at a loss to offset capital gains and reduce taxes.

**Technical Analysis:** Analyzing securities using price charts, patterns, and indicators.

**Theta:** An options Greek measuring the rate of time decay in an option's value.

**Time Value (Options):** The portion of an option's premium beyond intrinsic value, based on time until expiration.

**TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities):** Government bonds that adjust principal for inflation.

**Total Return:** Investment return including capital appreciation, dividends, and interest.

**Traditional IRA/401(k):** Retirement accounts funded with pre-tax dollars; withdrawals are taxed as income.

**Trailing Stop:** A stop-loss order that moves with the price to lock in profits.

**Treasury Bond/Bill/Note:** Debt securities issued by the U.S. government with varying maturities.

---

## U

**Underlying Asset:** The security on which a derivative (like an option) is based.

**Undervalued:** A security trading below its perceived intrinsic value.

---

## V

**Valuation:** The process of determining the current worth of an asset or company.

**Value Investing:** An investment strategy seeking undervalued securities.

**Value Stock:** A stock that trades at a lower price relative to its fundamentals (earnings, dividends, book value).

**Vega:** An options Greek measuring sensitivity to changes in implied volatility.

**Vesting:** The process by which employees earn the right to employer contributions or benefits over time.

**VIX:** The CBOE Volatility Index, often called the "fear index"—measures expected market volatility.

**Volatility:** The degree of variation in an investment's price over time.

**Volume:** The number of shares or contracts traded in a given period.

---

## W

**Wash Sale Rule:** IRS rule prohibiting tax deduction on a loss if substantially identical securities are purchased within 30 days.

**Withdrawal Rate:** The percentage of a portfolio withdrawn annually in retirement.

---

## Y

**Yield:** The income return on an investment, expressed as a percentage.

**Yield Curve:** A graph showing interest rates of bonds with equal credit quality but different maturities.

---

## Z

**Zero-Coupon Bond:** A bond that pays no periodic interest but is sold at a discount to face value.
