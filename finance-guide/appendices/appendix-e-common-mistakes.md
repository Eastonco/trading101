# Appendix E: Common Mistakes to Avoid

A comprehensive list of mistakes at every experience level. Review periodically as a reminder.

---

## Beginner Mistakes

### 1. Not Starting

**The mistake:** Waiting for the "right time" to invest.

**Why it happens:** Fear of market timing, analysis paralysis, perfectionism.

**The truth:** Time in the market beats timing the market. Starting imperfectly beats not starting.

**The fix:** Open an account today. Start with any amount. Improve over time.

---

### 2. Not Having an Emergency Fund

**The mistake:** Investing before having 3-6 months of expenses saved.

**Why it happens:** Excitement about returns, underestimating risks.

**The consequence:** Forced to sell investments at bad times during emergencies.

**The fix:** Build emergency fund first, then invest. Non-negotiable.

---

### 3. Waiting to Invest Until You "Understand Everything"

**The mistake:** Delaying investing while trying to learn everything first.

**Why it happens:** Perfectionism, overwhelming amount of information.

**The truth:** You learn by doing. Basics are enough to start.

**The fix:** Learn the basics, start with index funds, learn more as you go.

---

### 4. Checking Investments Too Often

**The mistake:** Looking at your portfolio daily or multiple times per day.

**Why it happens:** Excitement, anxiety, addiction to the dopamine.

**The consequence:** Emotional decisions, unnecessary stress, potential overtrading.

**The fix:** Check monthly or quarterly. Remove apps from your phone if needed.

---

### 5. Ignoring Tax-Advantaged Accounts

**The mistake:** Investing in taxable accounts before maxing tax-advantaged options.

**Why it happens:** Confusion about account types, wanting immediate access.

**The cost:** Potentially tens of thousands in lifetime taxes.

**The fix:** Max 401(k) to match → IRA → HSA → More 401(k) → Then taxable.

---

### 6. Not Taking the Full Employer Match

**The mistake:** Not contributing enough to 401(k) to get the full employer match.

**Why it happens:** Prioritizing current spending, not understanding the value.

**The truth:** This is literally free money. 100% immediate return.

**The fix:** Contribute at least enough to get the full match. Always.

---

### 7. Investing Money You'll Need Soon

**The mistake:** Putting short-term savings (house down payment, car) in stocks.

**Why it happens:** Wanting higher returns, not understanding volatility.

**The consequence:** Market drops right when you need the money.

**The fix:** Money needed within 3-5 years belongs in savings accounts or short-term bonds.

---

## Intermediate Mistakes

### 8. Chasing Past Performance

**The mistake:** Buying investments because they did well recently.

**Why it happens:** Recency bias, assumption that winners keep winning.

**The truth:** Past performance does not predict future performance.

**The fix:** Stick to your allocation strategy. Don't chase last year's winners.

---

### 9. Trying to Time the Market

**The mistake:** Moving to cash when markets seem high, waiting to buy when they seem low.

**Why it happens:** Overconfidence, thinking you can predict markets.

**The truth:** Even professionals can't consistently time markets. Miss the 10 best days and you miss most of the gains.

**The fix:** Stay invested. Use dollar-cost averaging. Ignore the noise.

---

### 10. Panic Selling During Downturns

**The mistake:** Selling stocks during market crashes.

**Why it happens:** Fear, loss aversion, media panic.

**The consequence:** Locks in losses, misses recovery.

**The fix:** Have a plan before crashes happen. Review 2008, 2020 recoveries. Don't sell.

---

### 11. Not Diversifying

**The mistake:** Too much in one stock, sector, or asset class.

**Common examples:**
- 50%+ in employer stock
- All tech stocks
- All U.S. stocks, no international

**The fix:** Broad market index funds. Multiple asset classes. Geographic diversification.

---

### 12. Overdiversifying (Diworsification)

**The mistake:** Owning so many funds they overlap significantly or dilute returns.

**Example:** Owning 15 different funds that all hold the same large-cap stocks.

**The truth:** 3-5 funds can provide complete diversification.

**The fix:** Check fund holdings for overlap. Consolidate to core positions.

---

### 13. Ignoring Fees

**The mistake:** Not realizing how fees compound over time.

**Example:** 1% fee vs 0.03% fee over 30 years on $100,000:
- 0.03% fee: Pays ~$900 total
- 1.00% fee: Pays ~$30,000+ total

**The fix:** Use low-cost index funds. Compare expense ratios.

---

### 14. Not Rebalancing

**The mistake:** Letting winners become oversized portions of portfolio.

**Example:** Starting 60/40 stocks/bonds → After bull market: 80/20.

**The consequence:** Taking more risk than intended. Buying high, never selling.

**The fix:** Annual rebalancing. 5% drift triggers.

---

### 15. Wrong Asset Location

**The mistake:** Holding tax-inefficient investments in taxable accounts.

**Example:** REITs and bonds in taxable accounts instead of IRAs.

**The consequence:** Higher tax bills than necessary.

**The fix:** Bonds/REITs in traditional accounts. Stocks in Roth and taxable.

---

## Advanced Mistakes

### 16. Overconfidence After Success

**The mistake:** Increasing risk after a winning streak, assuming skill.

**Why it happens:** Confusing luck with skill. Ego.

**The consequence:** Bigger losses when luck runs out.

**The fix:** Systematic position sizing regardless of recent results. Humility.

---

### 17. Strategy Hopping

**The mistake:** Switching strategies after every drawdown.

**Pattern:**
1. Strategy underperforms for 6 months
2. Switch to new strategy
3. Old strategy starts working
4. New strategy now underperforms
5. Repeat

**The fix:** Commit to a strategy for years, not months. Evaluate on full cycles.

---

### 18. Optimizing for the Wrong Things

**The mistake:** Spending hours optimizing small details while ignoring big decisions.

**Example:** Agonizing over 0.03% vs 0.04% expense ratio while not maxing 401(k).

**The fix:** Focus on: savings rate, asset allocation, not selling in panics. The rest is rounding error.

---

### 19. Not Having a Sell Discipline

**The mistake:** Knowing when to buy but not when to sell.

**Consequence:** Riding winners down, holding losers forever.

**The fix:** Define sell criteria before buying. Rebalance systematically.

---

### 20. Letting Taxes Drive Every Decision

**The mistake:** Refusing to sell because of taxes, even when you should.

**Example:** Holding concentrated position despite risk because of capital gains.

**The truth:** Taxes matter, but risk management matters more.

**The fix:** Balance tax efficiency with proper diversification and risk management.

---

## Trading-Specific Mistakes

### 21. Trading Without a Plan

**The mistake:** Entering trades without defined entry, exit, and position size.

**Why it happens:** Excitement, FOMO, impulsivity.

**The consequence:** Emotional decisions, no accountability, inconsistent results.

**The fix:** Write the plan before entering. Entry, stop, target, size. Every trade.

---

### 22. Not Using Stop Losses

**The mistake:** Entering trades without defined maximum loss.

**Why it happens:** Hope, not wanting to "lock in" losses.

**The consequence:** Small losses become account-destroying losses.

**The fix:** Every trade has a stop. No exceptions. Calculate before entry.

---

### 23. Revenge Trading

**The mistake:** Trading aggressively to recover losses.

**The pattern:**
1. Lose $500
2. Take bigger position to "make it back"
3. Lose $800
4. Take even bigger position
5. Blow up

**The fix:** Daily loss limits. Cooling-off periods after losses. Walk away.

---

### 24. Averaging Down Without a Plan

**The mistake:** Adding to losing positions without predetermined levels.

**Why it happens:** Belief it "has to bounce," not wanting to admit being wrong.

**The consequence:** Doubling down all the way to zero.

**The fix:** If averaging down, plan it in advance. Know maximum position size.

---

### 25. Overleveraging

**The mistake:** Using too much margin or options leverage.

**Why it happens:** Greed, overconfidence, "sure thing" mentality.

**The consequence:** Forced liquidation at worst times, amplified losses.

**The fix:** Use leverage sparingly if at all. Never risk more than you can afford to lose.

---

## Retirement-Specific Mistakes

### 26. Not Starting Early Enough

**The mistake:** Delaying retirement savings until later in career.

**The math:** Starting at 25 vs 35 with same contributions = ~2x the ending balance.

**The fix:** Start immediately. Even $50/month matters.

---

### 27. Taking Early Withdrawals

**The mistake:** Raiding retirement accounts before retirement.

**The cost:** Taxes + penalties + lost compound growth.

**Example:** $10,000 early withdrawal at 30 = ~$150,000 less at 65.

**The fix:** Treat retirement accounts as untouchable. Build emergency fund instead.

---

### 28. Being Too Conservative for Too Long

**The mistake:** Having too-conservative allocation in accumulation years.

**Example:** Being 30 years old with 50% bonds.

**The consequence:** Missing decades of growth.

**The fix:** Match allocation to timeline. You can handle volatility with 30+ years.

---

### 29. Not Adjusting Allocation With Age

**The mistake:** Maintaining aggressive allocation into retirement.

**Example:** Being 70 years old with 90% stocks.

**The risk:** Sequence of returns risk in early retirement.

**The fix:** Glide path to more conservative allocation approaching retirement.

---

### 30. Ignoring Required Minimum Distributions

**The mistake:** Not taking RMDs on time.

**The penalty:** 25% of the amount not withdrawn (reduced from 50% in 2023).

**The fix:** Know your RMD start age. Take by December 31 each year. Set reminders.

---

## Behavioral Mistakes

### 31. Following the Crowd at Extremes

**The mistake:** Maximum bullishness at tops, maximum bearishness at bottoms.

**The pattern:** Feeling most confident right before crashes, most scared right before rallies.

**The fix:** Be skeptical of consensus. Extreme sentiment often marks turning points.

---

### 32. Anchoring to Entry Price

**The mistake:** Making decisions based on what you paid, not current value.

**Example:** "I can't sell until I'm back to breakeven."

**The truth:** Your entry price is irrelevant to future returns.

**The fix:** Ask: "Would I buy this today at this price?" If no, consider selling.

---

### 33. Holding Losers, Selling Winners

**The mistake:** Loss aversion causing inverted risk management.

**The pattern:** Sell stocks up 10%, hold stocks down 50%.

**The consequence:** Portfolio of losers.

**The fix:** Systematic selling rules. Ignore entry price. Evaluate on forward merit.

---

### 34. Confirmation Bias

**The mistake:** Only seeking information that supports your position.

**The pattern:** Long a stock → Only read bullish articles → Surprised by bad news.

**The fix:** Actively seek opposing views. Write the bear case for every bull position.

---

### 35. Overcomplicating Everything

**The mistake:** Believing complex is better than simple.

**The truth:** Simple strategies often outperform. Complexity creates more failure points.

**The fix:** Start simple. Add complexity only when truly necessary. Most don't need it.

---

## Quick Reference: Mistake Checklist

### Before Investing

- [ ] Emergency fund established?
- [ ] High-interest debt paid off?
- [ ] Employer match captured?
- [ ] Investment timeline defined?
- [ ] Risk tolerance honestly assessed?

### When Investing

- [ ] Using tax-advantaged accounts first?
- [ ] Keeping fees low?
- [ ] Diversified across asset classes?
- [ ] Written investment plan?
- [ ] Not checking too often?

### When Trading

- [ ] Entry, exit, and position size defined before trade?
- [ ] Stop loss in place?
- [ ] Risk per trade under 1-2%?
- [ ] Not revenge trading?
- [ ] Not overleveraged?

### Ongoing

- [ ] Rebalancing periodically?
- [ ] Not chasing past performance?
- [ ] Staying invested through downturns?
- [ ] Not strategy hopping?
- [ ] Continuing to learn (but not overcomplicating)?

---

## The Most Important Rule

**The single biggest mistake:** Not investing at all.

Every other mistake can be corrected over time. But time you don't spend invested cannot be recovered. Compound growth only works if you participate.

Start. Stay invested. Keep improving. Everything else is details.
