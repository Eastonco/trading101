# Appendix D: Recommended Resources

A curated list of quality resources for continuing your financial education.

---

## Books: Foundational

These books provide essential knowledge every investor should have.

### For Beginners

**"The Simple Path to Wealth" by JL Collins**
- The clearest case for index investing
- Accessible and entertaining
- Best for: Anyone starting out

**"I Will Teach You to Be Rich" by Ramit Sethi**
- Practical automation systems
- Behavioral focus
- Best for: Young professionals getting started

**"The Bogleheads' Guide to Investing" by Larimore, Lindauer, LeBoeuf**
- Comprehensive introduction
- Evidence-based approach
- Best for: DIY investors wanting a solid foundation

### Core Investment Knowledge

**"A Random Walk Down Wall Street" by Burton Malkiel**
- Classic case for passive investing
- Academic research made accessible
- Best for: Understanding why markets are hard to beat

**"The Intelligent Investor" by Benjamin Graham**
- Foundation of value investing
- Buffett's recommended book
- Best for: Understanding investment philosophy (read with commentary edition)

**"Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits" by Philip Fisher**
- Quality and growth investing
- Scuttlebutt method
- Best for: Stock pickers wanting to evaluate companies

**"Stocks for the Long Run" by Jeremy Siegel**
- Historical market analysis
- Long-term perspective
- Best for: Understanding asset class returns over time

### Behavioral Finance and Psychology

**"The Psychology of Money" by Morgan Housel**
- Relationship between money and behavior
- Story-driven, highly readable
- Best for: Understanding why we make money mistakes

**"Thinking, Fast and Slow" by Daniel Kahneman**
- Nobel laureate on cognitive biases
- Foundational behavioral economics
- Best for: Deep understanding of decision-making

**"Trading in the Zone" by Mark Douglas**
- Trading psychology classic
- Mental discipline
- Best for: Active traders struggling with emotions

### Portfolio Construction

**"The Four Pillars of Investing" by William Bernstein**
- Theory, history, psychology, business of investing
- Academic but accessible
- Best for: Serious investors wanting depth

**"All About Asset Allocation" by Rick Ferri**
- Comprehensive allocation guide
- Practical implementation
- Best for: Building diversified portfolios

### What to Avoid

**Most financial social media**
- Performance claims often fake
- Survivorship bias extreme
- Promotes short-term trading

**"Finfluencers" without credentials**
- Often selling courses
- Unverified track records
- Entertainment over education

**Paid stock picking services**
- Most don't outperform
- Expensive
- Creates dependency

## Building Your Resource System

### Recommended Approach

**Start with:**
1. One foundational book (Simple Path to Wealth or Bogleheads Guide)
2. Investopedia for terms you don't know
3. Bogleheads forum for specific questions

**Add over time:**
1. One or two quality podcasts
2. Portfolio Visualizer for analysis
3. Specialized books for topics you're pursuing

**Avoid:**
- Information overload
- Following too many sources
- Acting on every piece of advice

### Quality Filters

Before trusting a source, ask:
- Is this evidence-based or opinion?
- Does the author have relevant credentials?
- Is there a conflict of interest (selling something)?
- Does this align with academic research?
- Would I still trust this advice in 10 years?

---

*Remember: More sources doesn't mean better decisions. A few quality resources, deeply understood, beats hundreds of superficial ones.*
