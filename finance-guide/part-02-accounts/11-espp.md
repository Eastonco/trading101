# Chapter 11: Employee Stock Purchase Plans (ESPPs)

## Overview

An Employee Stock Purchase Plan (ESPP) allows employees to purchase company stock at a discount, typically 15% off market price. In the best-case scenarios, ESPPs can generate 40%+ instant returns with minimal risk—making them one of the best benefits employers offer.

This chapter covers how ESPPs work, the tax implications, and strategies to maximize this benefit.

---

## How ESPPs Work

### Basic Structure

1. **Enrollment:** You elect to participate during an enrollment window
2. **Payroll deductions:** A percentage of your salary is withheld each pay period
3. **Purchase period:** Typically 6 months of accumulating contributions
4. **Purchase:** At period end, company buys stock for you at a discount
5. **Receipt:** Shares deposited into your account

### Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Offering Period | The full period covered by one ESPP offer (6-24 months) |
| Purchase Period | When stock is actually purchased (often 6 months) |
| Grant Date | When the offering period begins |
| Purchase Date | When stock is purchased |
| Discount | Percentage off market price (usually 15%) |
| Lookback | Uses lower of grant date or purchase date price |

---

## The Discount

### Standard 15% Discount

Most ESPPs offer a 15% discount—the maximum allowed under Section 423 (qualified ESPP).

**Example:**
- Stock price on purchase date: $100
- Purchase price (15% discount): $85
- Instant gain: $15 (17.6% return)

### The Lookback Provision

Many ESPPs include a "lookback" feature that uses the **lower** of:
- Stock price at grant date (beginning of offering period)
- Stock price at purchase date

**Example with lookback:**
- Stock price at grant date: $80
- Stock price at purchase date: $120
- 15% discount applies to lower price ($80)
- You pay: $68
- Stock worth: $120
- **Instant gain: $52 (76.5% return!)**

### Even Better: If Stock Falls

**Example with declining stock:**
- Stock price at grant date: $100
- Stock price at purchase date: $80
- 15% discount on lower price ($80)
- You pay: $68
- Stock worth: $80
- **Instant gain: $12 (17.6% return)**

With lookback, you're protected on the downside—you always get at least the 15% discount.

---

## Contribution Limits

### IRS Limit

The IRS limits ESPP purchases to $25,000 in stock value per calendar year (based on grant date fair market value).

### Employer Limits

Most plans allow 1-15% of salary contributions. Some cap at 10%.

### Practical Limits

If your salary is $100,000 and you contribute 10%:
- Annual contribution: $10,000
- At 15% discount, you're buying ~$11,765 in stock
- Well under $25,000 IRS limit

---

## Qualified vs. Non-Qualified ESPPs

### Qualified (Section 423) Plans

Most ESPPs are qualified, offering:
- Maximum 15% discount
- Favorable tax treatment for qualifying dispositions
- Lookback allowed (up to 27 months)
- Cannot discriminate among employees
- $25,000 annual limit

### Non-Qualified Plans

Less common, may offer:
- Greater than 15% discounts
- Different terms for different employees
- Less favorable tax treatment

---

## Tax Treatment

ESPP taxation is complex. The timing of your sale determines tax treatment.

### Key Dates

| Date | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Grant Date | Offering period begins |
| Purchase Date | Stock purchased |
| Sale Date | When you sell the stock |

### Qualifying vs. Disqualifying Dispositions

**Qualifying Disposition:**
- Sold at least 2 years after grant date
- AND at least 1 year after purchase date

**Disqualifying Disposition:**
- Sold before meeting both holding requirements

### Tax Treatment Comparison

**Disqualifying Disposition:**
- Discount portion: Taxed as ordinary income
- Additional gain: Short-term or long-term capital gain (depending on holding period)

**Qualifying Disposition:**
- More favorable treatment, but complex
- Generally results in more long-term capital gains treatment

### Practical Example

**Setup:**
- Grant date price: $100
- Purchase date price: $120
- Your purchase price: $85 (15% discount on $100 due to lookback)
- You sell at: $130

**Disqualifying Disposition (sold within 1 year):**
- Ordinary income: $35 ($120 - $85 = actual discount received)
- Short-term capital gain: $10 ($130 - $120)

**Qualifying Disposition (sold after 2 years from grant, 1 year from purchase):**
- Ordinary income: $15 (15% of $100 grant price)
- Long-term capital gain: $30 ($130 - $100)

The qualifying disposition often results in lower total tax.

## ESPP Strategies

### Strategy 1: Sell Immediately

**How it works:**
- Buy shares at discount
- Sell same day or ASAP
- Lock in guaranteed profit

**Pros:**
- Guaranteed profit (the discount)
- No market risk after purchase
- Reduces concentration in company stock

**Cons:**
- Disqualifying disposition (higher tax on discount)
- Transaction costs/spreads
- Potential short-term capital gains

**Best for:** Risk-averse investors, those already concentrated in company stock

### Strategy 2: Hold for Qualifying Disposition

**How it works:**
- Buy shares at discount
- Hold until qualifying disposition requirements met
- Sell for potentially better tax treatment

**Pros:**
- More favorable tax treatment
- Potential for additional appreciation

**Cons:**
- Market risk during holding period
- Concentration risk in company stock
- Tax complexity

**Best for:** Those bullish on company, wanting tax optimization

### Strategy 3: Hybrid Approach

**How it works:**
- Sell most shares immediately
- Hold some for potential appreciation/qualifying disposition
- Limits downside while maintaining some upside

### Which Strategy Is Best?

**The math:** Even with disqualifying disposition tax treatment, selling immediately typically provides excellent risk-adjusted returns. A guaranteed 15%+ return in 6 months is phenomenal.

**The risk:** Holding exposes you to market risk. Your company's stock could drop significantly while waiting for qualifying disposition.

**My view:** For most people, selling immediately and reinvesting in diversified funds is optimal. Don't let tax tail wag the investment dog.

---

## Concentration Risk Warning

### The Enron Problem

Employees who held too much company stock in Enron, Lehman Brothers, WorldCom, and countless other companies lost both their jobs AND their retirement savings simultaneously.

### Double Jeopardy

If your company struggles:
- You might lose your job (employment income risk)
- Your company stock plummets (investment risk)

Both risks are correlated. Diversification protects you.

### Rule of Thumb

Keep company stock under 10% of your total portfolio. This includes:
- ESPP shares
- Company stock in 401(k)
- Stock options/RSUs
- Direct stock purchases

## ESPP vs. Other Benefits

### ESPP Return Analysis

Assuming 15% discount with 6-month purchase period:
- Contribution: $5,000 over 6 months
- Average cash tied up: ~$2,500 (building up over time)
- Stock purchased: ~$5,882 value
- Profit: $882
- Return on average capital: ~35% (annualized)

That's an exceptional guaranteed return.

### Priority in Benefits

1. **401(k) to employer match** - Free money
2. **Max HSA** - Triple tax advantage
3. **Max ESPP** - Guaranteed 15%+ return
4. **Max Roth IRA** - Tax-free growth
5. **Max 401(k)** - Tax-advantaged growth

ESPP is high priority due to guaranteed returns.

## Administrative Considerations

### Enrollment Windows

ESPPs typically have specific enrollment periods. If you miss one, you may wait 6 months for the next opportunity.

### Cash Flow Impact

Contributions reduce your take-home pay. Budget accordingly:
- 10% contribution = 10% less in each paycheck
- Plan for 6-month commitment

### Record Keeping

Keep records of:
- Grant dates and prices
- Purchase dates and prices
- Your cost basis
- Sale dates and prices

Your broker should provide this, but maintain your own records for tax purposes.

### Form 3922

Employer must provide Form 3922 showing ESPP transactions. Use this for tax reporting.

---

## Special Situations

### Leaving the Company

If you leave mid-offering:
- Accumulated contributions returned (usually no interest)
- No penalty
- You lose the discount opportunity for that period

### Stock Splits

If company stock splits during offering period, your purchase price adjusts accordingly.

### Multiple Offering Periods

Some plans have overlapping offering periods. You might be participating in multiple offerings simultaneously.

---

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Not Participating

Free money left on the table. Even 1% contribution captures some discount.

### 2. Not Contributing the Maximum

If you can afford it, max out ESPP contributions. The guaranteed return is exceptional.

### 3. Holding Too Long

Waiting for qualifying disposition while stock drops 30% wipes out any tax benefit.

### 4. Ignoring Concentration Risk

Too much company stock is dangerous. Diversify regularly.

### 5. Bad Record Keeping

ESPP tax reporting is complex. Poor records lead to overpaying taxes.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **ESPP offers 15%+ instant returns** - One of the best employee benefits
- **Lookback provision** can generate 40%+ returns if stock appreciates
- **Sell immediately for most people** - Lock in guaranteed profit, avoid concentration risk
- **Holding for qualifying disposition** saves taxes but adds market risk
- **Keep company stock under 10%** of total portfolio
- **Don't miss enrollment windows** - Participate at the maximum you can afford
- **Keep good records** - Complex tax reporting requires documentation
