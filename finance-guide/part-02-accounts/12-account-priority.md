# Chapter 12: Account Priority & Tax Optimization

## Overview

You now understand the major investment accounts: brokerage, 401(k), IRA, HSA, 529, and ESPP. The question becomes: In what order should you fund them? And where should you hold different types of investments?

This chapter provides a framework for prioritizing contributions and optimizing asset location across accounts to minimize taxes and maximize wealth.

---

## The Priority Framework

### The Optimal Order

Here's the generally recommended order for funding accounts:

**Tier 1: The Essentials**
1. Emergency fund (3-6 months expenses)
2. 401(k) to employer match (free money)

**Tier 2: Tax-Advantaged Growth**
3. HSA maximum (if eligible)
4. ESPP maximum (if available)
5. Roth IRA maximum

**Tier 3: Additional Tax-Advantaged**
6. 401(k) maximum (beyond match)
7. Mega Backdoor Roth (if available)
8. 529 for education goals

**Tier 4: Taxable**
9. Taxable brokerage account

### Why This Order?

| Priority | Account | Rationale |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Emergency fund | Financial foundation; prevents costly mistakes |
| 2 | 401(k) to match | 50-100% instant return on employer match |
| 3 | HSA | Triple tax advantage; best long-term vehicle |
| 4 | ESPP | 15%+ guaranteed return |
| 5 | Roth IRA | Tax-free growth forever; flexibility |
| 6 | 401(k) max | Tax-deferred growth; high contribution limit |
| 7 | Mega Backdoor | Additional Roth contributions if available |
| 8 | 529 | Tax-free for education; state deduction bonus |
| 9 | Taxable | No limits; full flexibility |

## Detailed Breakdown

### Step 1: Emergency Fund

**Before investing anything**, build a financial cushion.

**Target:** 3-6 months of essential expenses

**Where to keep it:**
- High-yield savings account (4-5% in 2024)
- Money market fund
- Not invested in stocks

**Why first?** Without emergency savings:
- You may need to sell investments at bad times
- You may take on high-interest debt
- Financial stress affects decision-making

### Step 2: 401(k) to Employer Match

**Never leave free money on the table.**

**Example:**
- Salary: $80,000
- Employer matches 50% of first 6%
- You contribute 6% ($4,800)
- Employer adds $2,400
- **Total: $7,200 (50% instant return)**

Even if your 401(k) has poor investment options, the match makes it worthwhile.

### Step 3: Max HSA

If you're eligible (HDHP), the HSA is arguably the best retirement account.

**Why so high priority?**
- Only account with triple tax advantage
- $4,150/$8,300 limit is "use it or lose it" opportunity
- Can be used as stealth retirement account
- No income limits

**Strategy:** Invest HSA funds, pay medical expenses out-of-pocket, reimburse yourself decades later.

### Step 4: Max ESPP

**Guaranteed 15%+ returns are rare.** If your employer offers ESPP:

- Contribute the maximum allowed (typically 10-15% of salary)
- Sell shares immediately to lock in profit
- Reinvest proceeds in diversified portfolio

**Math:** 15% discount over 6 months = 30%+ annualized return before tax

### Step 5: Max Roth IRA

**Why Roth IRA before maxing 401(k)?**

1. **Better investment options** - You choose any fund
2. **Lower fees** - Often cheaper than 401(k) options
3. **More flexibility** - Contributions withdrawable anytime
4. **No RMDs** - Money grows tax-free forever
5. **Tax diversification** - Balances Traditional 401(k)

**Income limits apply:** Use Backdoor Roth if over limits.

### Step 6: Max 401(k)

After capturing match and funding HSA/ESPP/Roth IRA, return to 401(k).

**$23,000 limit (2024)** provides substantial tax-advantaged space.

**Traditional vs. Roth 401(k):** Consider splitting to create tax diversification.

### Step 7: Mega Backdoor Roth

If your 401(k) plan allows:
1. After-tax contributions above $23,000
2. In-plan Roth conversions or rollovers

**Potential additional Roth savings:** Up to $46,000+ more per year.

Not all plans offer this. Check with HR.

### Step 8: 529 Plans

If you have education savings goals:
- Start early for maximum compound growth
- Capture state tax deduction if available
- Now has Roth IRA rollover escape valve

**Priority depends on:** Children's ages, education goals, state deduction value.

### Step 9: Taxable Brokerage

After exhausting tax-advantaged space:
- No contribution limits
- Full flexibility
- Step-up in basis at death

**Use tax-efficient investments:** Index ETFs, individual stocks (control timing), municipal bonds.

## Asset Location Strategy

Where you hold investments matters as much as what you hold.

### The Concept

Different investments have different tax characteristics. Placing them in the right accounts reduces taxes.

### General Guidelines

**Tax-Advantaged Accounts (401k, Traditional IRA):**
Best for tax-inefficient investments:
- Bonds (interest taxed as ordinary income)
- REITs (dividends taxed as ordinary income)
- Actively managed funds (frequent capital gains)
- High-turnover strategies

**Roth Accounts (Roth IRA, Roth 401k):**
Best for highest expected growth:
- Growth stocks
- Small-cap stocks
- Investments you want tax-free forever
- Highest appreciation potential

**Taxable Accounts:**
Best for tax-efficient investments:
- Index ETFs (low turnover)
- Individual stocks (control when to sell)
- Municipal bonds (tax-free interest)
- Tax-managed funds
- Long-term holdings

### Example Asset Location

**Portfolio goal:** 60% US Stocks, 25% International, 15% Bonds

| Account | Holdings | Rationale |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| 401(k) Traditional | Bonds, Int'l stocks | Bonds taxed at ordinary rates anyway |
| Roth IRA | US small-cap, growth | Highest growth potential; never taxed |
| HSA | Growth stocks | Tax-free for medical; growth maximizes benefit |
| Taxable | US total market ETF | Tax-efficient; low turnover |

### Don't Over-Complicate

Asset location provides marginal benefits. If it's too complex:
- Simple approach: Put everything in target-date funds
- Focus on asset allocation first
- Location optimization is secondary

## Tax Diversification

### The Uncertainty Problem

We don't know future tax rates. Having money in different tax treatments provides flexibility.

### Three Tax Buckets

1. **Tax-deferred (Traditional 401k/IRA):** Taxed on withdrawal
2. **Tax-free (Roth):** Never taxed
3. **Taxable (Brokerage):** Capital gains rates

### Benefits of Diversification

- Withdraw from Traditional up to top of current bracket
- Withdraw from Roth for additional needs (tax-free)
- Use taxable for specific purposes (long-term gains rates)
- Manage Medicare IRMAA thresholds
- Optimize for Social Security taxation

### Target Mix

By retirement, consider having substantial balances in all three buckets. This gives you flexibility regardless of future tax policy.

## Roth Conversion Strategy

### Converting Traditional to Roth

You can convert Traditional IRA/401(k) to Roth at any time, paying taxes on the conversion.

### When to Convert

**Good times:**
- Low-income years (job change, early retirement)
- Market downturns (convert depressed assets)
- Before RMDs begin
- Years with large deductions
- To fill up lower tax brackets

**Be cautious:**
- Large conversions push you into higher brackets
- Can trigger Medicare premium surcharges (IRMAA)
- Need cash to pay taxes (don't use converted funds)

### Bracket-Filling Strategy

**Example (Single filer, 2024):**
- Current income: $60,000
- 22% bracket ends at: $100,525
- Room to convert: $40,525 at 22% rate

Convert exactly enough to fill the bracket, avoiding the 24% rate.

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Skipping Employer Match

The 50-100% instant return beats any other investment. Always get the full match.

### 2. Ignoring HSA

Many people use HSA as a spending account rather than the powerful retirement vehicle it can be.

### 3. All Traditional or All Roth

Tax diversification provides flexibility. Don't put all eggs in one basket.

### 4. Over-Contributing to 529

Max retirement accounts before 529. You can borrow for college; you can't borrow for retirement.

### 5. Taxable Before Tax-Advantaged

Every dollar in taxable accounts should come after maxing tax-advantaged options.

### 6. Ignoring Asset Location

Holding bonds in taxable and REITs in Roth costs you money over time.

## Key Takeaways

- **Emergency fund comes first** - Financial foundation before investing
- **Never skip employer match** - 50-100% instant return
- **HSA is underrated** - Triple tax advantage is unmatched
- **ESPP is excellent** - Guaranteed 15%+ returns
- **Roth IRA before maxing 401(k)** - Better options, more flexibility
- **Asset location matters** - Put tax-inefficient investments in tax-advantaged accounts
- **Tax diversification is valuable** - Have Traditional, Roth, and Taxable buckets
- **Max tax-advantaged before taxable** - Every dollar deserves tax shelter if possible
