# Chapter 59: Sample Portfolios by Life Stage

## Overview

Theory becomes real when you see actual portfolios. This chapter provides concrete examples of investment portfolios for different life stages, income levels, and risk tolerances. Use these as starting points—not prescriptions—for building your own portfolio. Every situation is unique, but seeing examples helps translate concepts into action.

---

## How to Use These Portfolios

### These Are Starting Points

**Customize based on:**
- Your specific goals and timeline
- Your risk tolerance (not just age)
- Your income stability
- Your other resources (pension, inheritance, etc.)
- Your tax situation

### What's Included

**Each portfolio shows:**
- Target allocation
- Specific fund suggestions
- Account placement (tax optimization)
- Expected returns and risk
- Rebalancing approach

### Important Caveats

**Remember:**
- Past performance doesn't guarantee future results
- Your situation may require adjustments
- Consult a professional for complex situations
- These are examples, not personalized advice

## Portfolio 1: Aggressive Growth (Ages 20-30)

**Typical situation:**
- Early career, income growing
- 30+ year time horizon to retirement
- High risk capacity (time to recover from downturns)

## Portfolio 8: Aggressive Trader

### Profile

**Warning: This approach is not suitable for most investors.**

**For investors who:**
- Have significant time to dedicate (10+ hours/week)
- Have proven trading track record
- Can handle significant volatility
- Have money they can afford to lose
- Understand most traders fail

### Core vs. Trading Allocation

| Portion | Allocation | Purpose |
|---------|------------|---------|
| Core Portfolio | 80% | Long-term wealth building |
| Trading Capital | 20% | Active trading |

### Core Portfolio (80%)

Standard index approach:
| Fund | Allocation |
|------|------------|
| US Total Stock | 50% |
| International | 30% |
| Bonds | 20% |

### Trading Capital (20%)

**Rules:**
- Never risk more than 20% of total portfolio
- Never risk more than 1% of total portfolio per trade
- Maintain strict stop losses
- Track every trade in journal

**Example with $100,000 total portfolio:**
- Core: $80,000 in index funds
- Trading: $20,000 active capital
- Max risk per trade: $1,000 (1% of total)

## Target-Date Fund Alternative

### For Those Who Want Simplicity

**Target-date funds do everything automatically:**
- Age-appropriate allocation
- Automatic rebalancing
- Gradual de-risking over time
- Single fund simplicity

### Example: Vanguard Target Retirement

| Fund | Age Range | Stock/Bond |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Target 2065 | Currently 20s | 90/10 |
| Target 2055 | Currently 30s | 90/10 |
| Target 2045 | Currently 40s | 85/15 |
| Target 2035 | Currently 50s | 75/25 |
| Target 2025 | Currently 60s | 55/45 |
| Target Income | Retired | 30/70 |

### Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Extremely simple
- Automatic rebalancing
- Appropriate glide path
- Low cost (0.08-0.15% at Vanguard)

**Cons:**
- No customization
- One-size-fits-all within age group
- Can't optimize tax location
- May not fit your specific situation

## Adapting to Your Situation

### Higher Risk Tolerance

**Adjustments:**
- Increase stock allocation by 10-20%
- Increase emerging markets allocation
- Reduce bond allocation
- Consider small-cap tilt

### Lower Risk Tolerance

**Adjustments:**
- Decrease stock allocation by 10-20%
- Increase bond allocation
- Add more short-term bonds
- Increase cash allocation

### Higher Income, Higher Tax Bracket

**Adjustments:**
- Add municipal bonds in taxable accounts
- Maximize tax-advantaged accounts
- Focus on tax-efficient funds in taxable
- Consider Roth conversions in lower-income years

### Self-Employed

**Adjustments:**
- Open Solo 401(k) or SEP IRA
- Potentially contribute $69,000+ annually
- May need larger cash reserve
- Consider disability insurance

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Match allocation to time horizon and risk tolerance** - Not just age
- **Simpler is often better** - 3-5 funds can build an excellent portfolio
- **Account location matters** - Tax-efficient placement improves returns
- **Adjust as life changes** - Review and update annually
- **Target-date funds work** - Great option if you want simplicity
- **Most investors should avoid "trading" portfolios** - Core indexing beats most traders
- **Your situation is unique** - Use examples as starting points, not prescriptions
- **Time in market beats timing** - Consistent contributions matter most
