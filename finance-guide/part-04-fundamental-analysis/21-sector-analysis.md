# Chapter 21: Sector Analysis

## Overview

The stock market is divided into sectors—groupings of companies with similar business activities. Different sectors perform differently depending on economic conditions, interest rates, and business cycles. Understanding sectors helps you diversify effectively and identify opportunities.

---

## The 11 GICS Sectors

The Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS) divides the market into 11 sectors:

| Sector | % of S&P 500 (approx) | Characteristics |
|--------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Technology | 29% | High growth, innovation |
| Healthcare | 13% | Defensive, demographics |
| Financials | 13% | Economic sensitive, interest rates |
| Consumer Discretionary | 10% | Cyclical, consumer confidence |
| Communication Services | 9% | Mix of growth and defensive |
| Industrials | 8% | Economic bellwether |
| Consumer Staples | 6% | Defensive, recession-resistant |
| Energy | 4% | Commodity-driven, volatile |
| Utilities | 3% | Defensive, income |
| Real Estate | 2% | Interest rate sensitive |
| Materials | 3% | Cyclical, commodity exposure |

## Sector Deep Dives

### Technology (Information Technology)

**What's included:** Software, hardware, semiconductors, IT services

**Key drivers:**
- Innovation and product cycles
- Enterprise IT spending
- Consumer device demand
- Cloud computing growth

**Characteristics:**
- High growth potential
- Higher valuations
- Lower dividends
- Can be volatile

**Key metrics:** Revenue growth, R&D spending, subscription metrics

**Examples:** Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Salesforce

### Healthcare

**What's included:** Pharmaceuticals, biotech, medical devices, healthcare providers, insurers

**Key drivers:**
- Aging demographics
- Drug pipelines
- Regulatory environment
- Healthcare policy

**Characteristics:**
- Relatively defensive
- Patent cliffs create risk
- R&D-intensive
- Long product cycles

**Key metrics:** Drug pipeline, patent expirations, revenue per drug

**Examples:** Johnson & Johnson, UnitedHealth, Pfizer, Abbott

### Financials

**What's included:** Banks, insurance, asset managers, exchanges

**Key drivers:**
- Interest rates (banks benefit from higher rates)
- Economic growth
- Credit quality
- Regulatory environment

**Characteristics:**
- Interest rate sensitive
- Cyclical
- Dividend payers
- Complex balance sheets

**Key metrics:** Net interest margin, book value, credit losses

**Examples:** JPMorgan, Berkshire Hathaway, Goldman Sachs, Visa

### Consumer Discretionary

**What's included:** Retail, restaurants, autos, hotels, apparel

**Key drivers:**
- Consumer confidence
- Employment
- Disposable income
- Housing wealth

**Characteristics:**
- Highly cyclical
- First to fall in recession
- First to recover in expansion
- E-commerce disruption

**Key metrics:** Same-store sales, consumer confidence, auto sales

**Examples:** Amazon, Tesla, Home Depot, Nike, McDonald's

### Communication Services

**What's included:** Telecom, media, entertainment, social media, advertising

**Key drivers:**
- Advertising spending
- Streaming competition
- 5G deployment
- User engagement

**Characteristics:**
- Mix of growth and defensive
- Restructured sector (formerly telecom)
- Content is key
- Platform dynamics

**Key metrics:** Subscribers, ARPU, engagement metrics

**Examples:** Alphabet, Meta, Netflix, Disney, Verizon

### Industrials

**What's included:** Aerospace, defense, machinery, transportation, construction

**Key drivers:**
- Economic growth
- Infrastructure spending
- Global trade
- Defense budgets

**Characteristics:**
- Economic bellwether
- Cyclical
- Capital-intensive
- Order backlog visibility

**Key metrics:** Orders, backlog, capacity utilization

**Examples:** Boeing, Caterpillar, Union Pacific, Honeywell

### Consumer Staples

**What's included:** Food, beverages, household products, tobacco

**Key drivers:**
- Population growth
- Consumer preferences
- Commodity costs
- Emerging market growth

**Characteristics:**
- Defensive
- Recession-resistant
- Stable dividends
- Lower growth
- Pricing power

**Key metrics:** Organic growth, market share, margins

**Examples:** Procter & Gamble, Coca-Cola, Walmart, Costco

### Energy

**What's included:** Oil & gas exploration, production, refining, equipment

**Key drivers:**
- Oil and gas prices
- Global demand
- OPEC policy
- Energy transition

**Characteristics:**
- Highly volatile
- Commodity-driven
- Cyclical
- High dividends (when profitable)

**Key metrics:** Production volumes, reserve replacement, breakeven prices

**Examples:** ExxonMobil, Chevron, Schlumberger

### Utilities

**What's included:** Electric, gas, water utilities, renewable power

**Key drivers:**
- Interest rates (bond proxy)
- Regulation
- Weather
- Clean energy transition

**Characteristics:**
- Most defensive sector
- High dividends
- Regulated returns
- Interest rate sensitive

**Key metrics:** Rate base growth, dividend yield, payout ratio

**Examples:** NextEra Energy, Duke Energy, Southern Company

### Real Estate (REITs)

**What's included:** Office, retail, residential, industrial, data centers, cell towers

**Key drivers:**
- Interest rates
- Economic growth
- Property values
- Occupancy rates

**Characteristics:**
- Income-focused
- Interest rate sensitive
- Tax-advantaged structure
- Diverse subsectors

**Key metrics:** FFO (Funds From Operations), occupancy, same-property NOI

**Examples:** Prologis, American Tower, Realty Income

### Materials

**What's included:** Chemicals, metals, mining, packaging, construction materials

**Key drivers:**
- Commodity prices
- Global economic growth
- Construction activity
- Currency movements

**Characteristics:**
- Cyclical
- Commodity exposure
- Global demand-driven
- Capital-intensive

**Key metrics:** Production costs, commodity prices, volume

**Examples:** Linde, Freeport-McMoRan, Sherwin-Williams

## Cyclical vs. Defensive Sectors

### Cyclical Sectors

**Outperform during:** Economic expansion, early recovery
**Underperform during:** Recession, late cycle

- Consumer Discretionary
- Industrials
- Materials
- Financials
- Energy
- Real Estate

### Defensive Sectors

**Outperform during:** Recession, market stress
**Underperform during:** Strong economic growth

- Consumer Staples
- Utilities
- Healthcare

### Mixed/Growth

- Technology (growth with cyclical elements)
- Communication Services (mix of defensive and growth)

## Sector Rotation

### What It Is

Moving portfolio allocation between sectors based on economic cycle.

### Business Cycle Positioning

| Cycle Phase | Favored Sectors | Avoided Sectors |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Early Recovery | Consumer Discretionary, Industrials, Financials | Utilities, Staples |
| Mid Expansion | Technology, Industrials, Materials | Utilities |
| Late Expansion | Energy, Materials | Consumer Discretionary |
| Recession | Utilities, Staples, Healthcare | Discretionary, Industrials |

### Practical Challenges

- Timing the cycle is difficult
- Sectors can move before obvious cycle turns
- Individual stock selection often matters more
- Transaction costs and taxes from frequent rotation

### Alternative Approach

Instead of rotating:
- Maintain broad diversification
- Modestly overweight favored sectors
- Use sector tilts rather than all-or-nothing bets

## Interest Rate Sensitivity

### Rate-Sensitive Sectors

**Benefit from higher rates:**
- Financials (banks earn more on deposits)

**Hurt by higher rates:**
- Utilities (bond-like, compete with bonds)
- Real Estate (higher borrowing costs)
- Growth stocks (future earnings worth less)

**Relatively insensitive:**
- Consumer Staples
- Healthcare

## Portfolio Sector Allocation

### Market-Weight Approach

Match S&P 500 sector weights. Get market return without sector bets.

### Active Tilts

Overweight sectors you're bullish on, underweight bearish sectors. Keep tilts modest (±5-10%).

### Concentration Limits

Consider limiting any single sector to 25-30% of portfolio to avoid concentration risk.

### Rebalancing

Sectors that outperform become larger weights. Rebalance periodically to maintain target allocation.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **11 GICS sectors** divide the market into meaningful groups
- **Cyclical sectors** move with the economy; defensive sectors resist
- **Interest rates affect sectors differently** - utilities hurt, banks helped
- **Sector rotation is difficult** - modest tilts beat dramatic shifts
- **Diversification across sectors reduces risk** - don't over-concentrate
- **Sector ETFs provide easy access** - low-cost, diversified exposure
- **Individual stock selection still matters** - sector is backdrop, not destiny
