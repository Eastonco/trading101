## Layer 1 Blockchains

### What Is a Layer 1?

**Layer 1 (L1) = The base blockchain itself** - The main network where transactions are settled.

**Examples:** Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, Cardano, Avalanche

### Bitcoin (BTC)

**Market position:** #1 by market cap
**Use case:** Store of value, digital gold
**Technology:** Proof of Work
**Strengths:** First mover, most secure, widest adoption
**Weaknesses:** Slow, limited programmability, energy-intensive

### Ethereum (ETH)

**Market position:** #2 by market cap
**Use case:** Programmable smart contracts, DeFi, NFTs
**Technology:** Proof of Stake (since 2022)
**Strengths:** Largest developer ecosystem, most dApps
**Weaknesses:** High gas fees during congestion, scalability challenges

### Solana (SOL)

**Market position:** Top 10
**Use case:** High-speed, low-cost transactions
**Technology:** Proof of Stake with Proof of History
**Strengths:** Fast (~400ms), cheap ($0.01 fees), growing ecosystem
**Weaknesses:** More centralized, network outages in past

### Cardano (ADA)

**Market position:** Top 10
**Use case:** Academic/research-driven smart contracts
**Technology:** Proof of Stake (Ouroboros)
**Strengths:** Peer-reviewed development, energy efficient
**Weaknesses:** Slower development, less DeFi adoption

### Other Notable L1s

| Blockchain | Focus | Key Feature |
|------------|-------|-------------|
| Avalanche (AVAX) | DeFi, Enterprise | Subnets (customizable blockchains) |
| Polkadot (DOT) | Interoperability | Parachains connect different blockchains |
| Cosmos (ATOM) | Interoperability | Inter-blockchain communication |
| Near Protocol | Developer-friendly | Sharding for scalability |
| Algorand (ALGO) | Speed, Finality | Instant transaction finality |

### The Layer 1 Competition

**The question:** Will one L1 win, or will multiple coexist?

**Arguments for consolidation:**
- Network effects favor one winner
- Liquidity concentrates
- Developer mindshare limited

**Arguments for multi-chain:**
- Different use cases need different tradeoffs
- No one blockchain is perfect
- Interoperability bridges emerging

## Layer 2 Scaling Solutions

### What Is a Layer 2?

**Layer 2 (L2) = Built on top of Layer 1** to improve speed/cost while inheriting security.

**Analogy:** L1 is the highway, L2 is an express lane built on top.

### How L2s Work

1. Transactions happen off the main chain (L2)
2. Periodically, L2 "settles" back to L1
3. L1 provides security and final settlement
4. Users get speed/cost benefits

### Ethereum L2s

**Optimistic Rollups:**
- Arbitrum, Optimism
- Assume transactions are valid, challenge period
- Lower fees, Ethereum security

**ZK-Rollups:**
- zkSync, StarkNet
- Use cryptographic proofs to verify
- Higher security guarantees

### Bitcoin L2s

**Lightning Network:**
- Payment channels for instant, cheap Bitcoin transactions
- Best for small, frequent payments
- Growing merchant adoption

### L2 Considerations

**Pros:**
- Much lower fees than L1
- Faster transactions
- Inherit L1 security

**Cons:**
- Bridge risk (moving between L1/L2)
- Complexity for users
- Ecosystem fragmentation

## Stablecoins

### What Are Stablecoins?

**Cryptocurrencies designed to maintain a stable value**, typically pegged to $1 USD.

### Why Stablecoins Exist

- Move fiat value on blockchain quickly
- Trading/DeFi without constant conversions
- Avoid volatility while staying in crypto
- Earn yield in "dollars"

### Types of Stablecoins

**Fiat-Collateralized:**
- Backed by actual dollars in bank accounts
- Examples: USDC (Circle), USDT (Tether)
- Redemption: $1 USDC → $1 actual

**Crypto-Collateralized:**
- Backed by other crypto (over-collateralized)
- Example: DAI (backed by ETH and other crypto)
- Decentralized, no central issuer

**Algorithmic:**
- Maintain peg through supply/demand mechanisms
- **HIGH RISK** - Several have failed catastrophically
- Example: UST collapse lost $40B in 2022

### Stablecoin Comparison

| Stablecoin | Type | Market Cap | Issuer |
|------------|------|------------|--------|
| USDT (Tether) | Fiat-backed | $80B+ | Tether Ltd |
| USDC (USD Coin) | Fiat-backed | $25B+ | Circle |
| DAI | Crypto-backed | $5B+ | MakerDAO |

### Stablecoin Risks

**Counterparty risk:** Is the backing actually there? (Tether questions)
**Regulatory risk:** Government crackdowns on issuers
**Depeg risk:** Can lose peg if trust erodes
**Smart contract risk:** For decentralized stablecoins

## DeFi Tokens

### What Is DeFi?

**Decentralized Finance (DeFi):** Financial services built on blockchain without traditional intermediaries.

### DeFi Categories and Tokens

**Decentralized Exchanges (DEXs):**
- Uniswap (UNI): Largest DEX, automated market maker
- SushiSwap (SUSHI): Uniswap fork with additional features
- Curve (CRV): Optimized for stablecoin swaps

**Lending/Borrowing:**
- Aave (AAVE): Flash loans, variable rates
- Compound (COMP): Earn interest on deposits
- MakerDAO (MKR): DAI stablecoin governance

**Derivatives:**
- dYdX: Perpetual futures trading
- GMX: Decentralized perpetual exchange
- Synthetix (SNX): Synthetic assets

### How DeFi Tokens Work

**Governance:** Token holders vote on protocol changes
**Fee sharing:** Some protocols share revenue with token holders
**Staking:** Lock tokens to earn rewards
**Utility:** Required for certain protocol functions

### DeFi Risks

**Smart contract risk:** Code bugs can drain funds
**Impermanent loss:** Providing liquidity can underperform holding
**Rug pulls:** Developers abandon/steal funds
**Complexity:** Easy to make expensive mistakes
**Regulatory uncertainty:** Future crackdowns possible

## Meme Coins

### What Are Meme Coins?

**Cryptocurrencies created as jokes or based on internet memes**, often with no utility beyond speculation.

### Notable Meme Coins

**Dogecoin (DOGE):**
- Started as joke (2013)
- Shiba Inu dog meme
- Promoted by Elon Musk
- No fixed supply

**Shiba Inu (SHIB):**
- "Dogecoin killer"
- Extremely low unit price (psychological appeal)
- Massive supply

**Pepe, Bonk, and others:**
- New meme coins launch constantly
- Most go to zero

### Why Meme Coins Are Risky

**No fundamental value:** No technology, no use case
**Extreme volatility:** 90%+ drops common
**Manipulation:** Easily pumped and dumped
**FOMO-driven:** Buy based on hype, not analysis
**Most fail:** 99%+ of meme coins go to zero

### If You Choose to Speculate

- Only use money you can lose 100%
- Size positions extremely small (<1% of portfolio)
- Take profits on the way up
- Never add to losing positions
- Recognize it's gambling, not investing

## NFTs (Conceptual Overview)

### What Are NFTs?

**Non-Fungible Tokens:** Unique digital assets verified on blockchain.

**Fungible:** One Bitcoin = any other Bitcoin (interchangeable)
**Non-fungible:** Each NFT is unique and distinguishable

### NFT Use Cases

**Digital art:** Ownership of digital artwork
**Collectibles:** Digital trading cards, profile pictures
**Gaming:** In-game items, characters
**Music:** Rights, royalties, exclusive content
**Real-world assets:** Tokenized property, tickets

### NFT Market Reality

**Peak:** 2021-2022 saw billions in sales
**Decline:** Most NFTs lost 90%+ of value
**Survivors:** Some projects maintain value
**Speculation:** Most buying was speculative, not utility-driven

### NFT Considerations

**If interested:**
- Understand you're buying digital collectibles
- Most will likely lose value
- Only buy what you genuinely want to own
- Beware of fake collections, scams
- High transaction fees for buying/selling

## Evaluating Crypto Projects

### Red Flags

**Warning signs of likely failure/scam:**
- Anonymous team
- Unrealistic promises (guaranteed returns)
- Pressure to buy quickly (FOMO tactics)
- No clear use case
- Token unlocks that benefit insiders
- Copy-paste code without innovation
- Celebrity endorsements as main selling point

### Due Diligence Checklist

**Team:**
- [ ] Who is behind the project? (Real identities)
- [ ] Track record and experience?
- [ ] Active development?

**Technology:**
- [ ] What problem does it solve?
- [ ] Is the technology novel or a copy?
- [ ] Code audited by reputable firms?

**Tokenomics:**
- [ ] What is the token supply schedule?
- [ ] Who holds the tokens? (Team, VCs, public)
- [ ] Are there upcoming unlocks?
- [ ] How does the token capture value?

**Community:**
- [ ] Active, genuine community?
- [ ] Developer activity (GitHub commits)?
- [ ] Partnerships and adoption?

## Market Cap Tiers

### Understanding Size

**Large cap (>$10B):** BTC, ETH, major L1s
- More established
- Lower percentage gains potential
- Lower (but still significant) risk

**Mid cap ($1B-$10B):** Established altcoins
- Higher growth potential
- Higher risk
- More volatile

**Small cap ($100M-$1B):** Emerging projects
- Much higher risk
- Many will fail
- Some may 10x+ (but most won't)

**Micro cap (<$100M):** Speculative
- Extremely high risk
- Most will fail completely
- Only for pure speculation
