# Chapter 36: Cryptocurrency Fundamentals

## Overview

Cryptocurrency represents a paradigm shift in how we think about money and value transfer. Built on blockchain technology, crypto assets operate outside traditional financial systems, offering both unprecedented opportunities and significant risks. This chapter explains the foundational concepts you need to understand before investing in or trading cryptocurrencies.

---

## What Is Cryptocurrency?

### Definition

**Cryptocurrency is a digital asset that uses cryptography for security and operates on a decentralized network** (typically a blockchain).

### Key Characteristics

**Decentralized:** No central authority (like a bank or government) controls it
**Digital:** Exists only electronically, no physical form
**Cryptographic:** Uses advanced mathematics to secure transactions
**Immutable:** Transactions cannot be reversed or altered
**Transparent:** All transactions are publicly viewable on the blockchain
**Scarce:** Most have limited supply (unlike fiat money)

### How It Differs from Traditional Money

| Aspect | Traditional Money | Cryptocurrency |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|
| Issuer | Central banks | Protocol/Network |
| Control | Government/Banks | Decentralized |
| Supply | Can be expanded | Often fixed or predictable |
| Transactions | Through intermediaries | Peer-to-peer |
| Privacy | Bank knows all transactions | Pseudonymous |
| Settlement | Days (wire transfers) | Minutes to hours |
| Operating hours | Business hours | 24/7/365 |

---

## How Blockchain Works

### The Basics

**A blockchain is a distributed ledger** that records all transactions across a network of computers.

**Key components:**
- **Blocks:** Containers of transaction data
- **Chain:** Blocks linked cryptographically
- **Nodes:** Computers that maintain the network
- **Consensus:** How the network agrees on truth

### Transaction Flow

1. **You initiate a transaction** (send Bitcoin to someone)
2. **Transaction broadcasts** to the network
3. **Nodes validate** the transaction
4. **Transaction enters a block** with others
5. **Block is verified** through consensus mechanism
6. **Block is added** to the chain permanently
7. **Recipient sees funds** in their wallet

### Why Blockchain Matters

**Security:** Altering past transactions is practically impossible
**Transparency:** Anyone can verify transactions
**Censorship-resistant:** No single entity can stop transactions
**Trustless:** Don't need to trust intermediaries

## Bitcoin: The First Cryptocurrency

### Origins

**Created:** 2008 whitepaper by "Satoshi Nakamoto" (pseudonymous)
**Launched:** January 2009
**Purpose:** Peer-to-peer electronic cash system

### Key Features

**Fixed supply:** 21 million Bitcoin maximum (ever)
**Mining:** New Bitcoin created through computational work
**Halving:** Mining rewards cut in half every ~4 years
**Settlement:** ~10 minute block time

### Bitcoin as Digital Gold

**Why the comparison:**
- Limited supply (scarcity)
- Difficult to produce (mining)
- Durable (can't be destroyed)
- Portable (easier than physical gold)
- Divisible (to 8 decimal places)

**Differences from gold:**
- No physical form
- Much more volatile
- Shorter track record
- Technology risk

## Ethereum: The Programmable Blockchain

### What Is Ethereum?

**Ethereum is a blockchain that supports smart contracts**—programmable code that executes automatically when conditions are met.

**Launched:** 2015 by Vitalik Buterin and others
**Native currency:** Ether (ETH)

### Smart Contracts

**Definition:** Self-executing contracts with terms written in code.

**Example:**
- Code: "If Party A sends $100 and Party B sends the widget, transfer both."
- Execution: Automatic when conditions met
- No intermediary: No escrow company needed

### What Ethereum Enables

**Decentralized applications (dApps):**
- DeFi (decentralized finance)
- NFTs (digital collectibles)
- DAOs (decentralized organizations)
- Decentralized exchanges

### Bitcoin vs. Ethereum

| Feature | Bitcoin | Ethereum |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Primary purpose | Store of value | Programmable platform |
| Supply cap | 21 million | No hard cap (but issuance reduced) |
| Block time | ~10 minutes | ~12 seconds |
| Consensus | Proof of Work | Proof of Stake |
| Smart contracts | Limited | Full functionality |

## Consensus Mechanisms

### Proof of Work (PoW)

**How it works:**
- Miners compete to solve complex math problems
- First to solve adds the block and gets rewarded
- Requires significant computational power
- Uses substantial electricity

**Used by:** Bitcoin, Litecoin, Dogecoin

**Pros:** Battle-tested, highly secure
**Cons:** Energy-intensive, slower, centralization risk in mining

### Proof of Stake (PoS)

**How it works:**
- Validators stake their coins as collateral
- Selected to create blocks based on stake size
- Bad behavior = lose staked coins (slashing)
- No mining hardware needed

**Used by:** Ethereum, Cardano, Solana

**Pros:** Energy efficient, potentially more scalable
**Cons:** Rich get richer dynamics, newer and less tested

## Crypto Wallets

### What Is a Wallet?

**A crypto wallet stores the private keys** that prove ownership of your cryptocurrency. The crypto itself stays on the blockchain.

### Hot Wallets (Connected to Internet)

**Types:**
- Exchange wallets (Coinbase, Binance)
- Software wallets (Exodus, MetaMask)
- Mobile wallets (Trust Wallet)

**Pros:** Convenient, easy to use, quick transactions
**Cons:** Vulnerable to hacks, exchange insolvency risk

### Cold Wallets (Offline Storage)

**Types:**
- Hardware wallets (Ledger, Trezor)
- Paper wallets (printed keys)
- Air-gapped computers

**Pros:** Maximum security, immune to online attacks
**Cons:** Less convenient, risk of physical loss/damage

### Wallet Security

**Critical concept:** Whoever controls the private keys controls the crypto.

**"Not your keys, not your coins"** - If your crypto is on an exchange, the exchange controls the keys. If the exchange is hacked or goes bankrupt, you may lose access.

### Seed Phrases

**Your seed phrase (recovery phrase) = Master key to your wallet**

- 12-24 words that can regenerate your wallet
- Never share with anyone
- Store offline in multiple secure locations
- If lost, your crypto is lost forever

## Market Structure

### How Crypto Markets Work

**24/7 trading:** Unlike stocks, crypto never closes
**Global:** Trades on exchanges worldwide
**Multiple exchanges:** Price can vary between exchanges
**Fractional:** Can buy tiny amounts (0.0001 BTC)

### Types of Exchanges

**Centralized exchanges (CEX):**
- Coinbase, Binance, Kraken
- Company operates the exchange
- Easier for beginners
- Custody risk (they hold your crypto)

**Decentralized exchanges (DEX):**
- Uniswap, SushiSwap, dYdX
- No central operator
- Trade directly from wallet
- More complex, higher gas fees

### Market Hours

| Market | Hours |
|--------|-------|
| US Stock Market | 9:30 AM - 4:00 PM ET (weekdays) |
| Crypto Market | 24/7/365 |

This creates unique dynamics:
- Weekend moves can be significant
- No "gap" risk like stocks
- Requires different monitoring approach

## Crypto Terminology

### Essential Terms

**HODL:** Hold on for dear life (hold long-term despite volatility)
**FOMO:** Fear of missing out
**FUD:** Fear, uncertainty, doubt
**Whale:** Large holder who can move markets
**Moon:** Significant price increase ("to the moon")
**Rekt:** Significant loss (wrecked)

### Technical Terms

**Market cap:** Price × Circulating supply
**Circulating supply:** Coins currently in circulation
**Total supply:** All coins created (including locked)
**Max supply:** Maximum that will ever exist
**Gas fees:** Transaction costs on Ethereum

### DeFi Terms

**TVL:** Total Value Locked (assets in DeFi protocols)
**Yield farming:** Earning returns by providing liquidity
**Staking:** Locking coins to earn rewards
**APY:** Annual Percentage Yield
**Liquidity pool:** Paired assets for trading

## Why People Invest in Crypto

### Bull Case

**Store of value:** Inflation hedge, "digital gold"
**Technology adoption:** Blockchain as transformative technology
**Financial inclusion:** Banking the unbanked globally
**Speculation:** High volatility creates trading opportunities
**Diversification:** Low correlation to traditional assets (sometimes)
**Early adoption:** Still early in adoption curve

### Bear Case / Risks

**Volatility:** 50-80% drawdowns are common
**Regulation:** Government crackdowns possible
**Technology risk:** Code bugs, hacks, failures
**No intrinsic value:** No cash flows like stocks
**Environmental concerns:** Energy usage (PoW)
**Scams:** Rug pulls, Ponzi schemes prevalent
**Lost access:** Forgotten passwords = lost forever

## Crypto as Part of a Portfolio

### Allocation Considerations

**Conservative:** 0-1% of portfolio
**Moderate:** 1-5% of portfolio
**Aggressive:** 5-10% of portfolio

**Important:** Only invest what you can afford to lose entirely.

### Risk Management

**Given extreme volatility:**
- Smaller position sizes than stocks
- Be prepared for 50%+ drawdowns
- Don't check prices hourly (it's 24/7)
- Have a plan before volatility hits

### Dollar-Cost Averaging

**Particularly useful for crypto:**
- Smooths out extreme volatility
- Removes timing pressure
- Builds position systematically
- Reduces emotional decisions

## Key Takeaways

- **Cryptocurrency is digital money on decentralized networks** secured by cryptography
- **Blockchain is the underlying technology** - A distributed, immutable ledger
- **Bitcoin is "digital gold"** - Store of value with fixed supply
- **Ethereum enables programmable applications** - Smart contracts and dApps
- **Security is your responsibility** - Learn about wallets and key management
- **Markets are 24/7** - Never closes, weekend moves can be significant
- **Volatility is extreme** - 50%+ drawdowns are normal
- **Only invest what you can afford to lose** - This isn't financial advice, it's survival advice
