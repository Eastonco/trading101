# Chapter 22: Long-Term Investing

## Overview

Long-term investing—buying quality assets and holding them for years or decades—is the approach most likely to build wealth for most people. It requires patience, discipline, and the ability to ignore short-term noise.

---

## The Case for Long-Term Investing

### Time Is Your Greatest Asset

**The math is compelling:**
- S&P 500 average annual return: ~10%
- $10,000 invested for 30 years at 10%: ~$175,000
- Active trading rarely beats this consistently

### Why It Works

1. **Compound returns:** Gains build on gains over time
2. **Lower costs:** Less trading = fewer fees and taxes
3. **Time smooths volatility:** Short-term noise matters less
4. **Aligns with business growth:** Companies build value over years, not days

### The Data Is Clear

Studies consistently show:
- Most active traders underperform buy-and-hold
- Most actively managed funds underperform index funds
- The more you trade, the worse you tend to do
- Time in market beats timing the market

## Core Principles

### Buy What You Understand

Warren Buffett's circle of competence concept:
- Invest in businesses you understand
- If you can't explain how a company makes money, don't invest
- Understanding helps you hold through volatility

### Focus on Quality

**Quality characteristics:**
- Durable competitive advantage (moat)
- Strong financials (low debt, high returns)
- Capable management
- Favorable industry dynamics
- Reasonable valuation

### Let Winners Run

**The math of big winners:**
- A few great investments drive most returns
- A stock can only lose 100%, but can gain 1,000%+
- Selling winners too early caps upside
- Trimming positions makes sense; complete exits often don't

### Don't Panic Sell

**Historical perspective:**
- The market has recovered from every crash
- Selling at the bottom locks in losses
- The best days often follow the worst days
- Missing the 10 best days dramatically hurts returns

## Investment Approaches

### Index Investing (Passive)

**What it is:** Buying funds that track market indices.

**Philosophy:** Don't try to beat the market; be the market.

**Advantages:**
- Lowest costs (0.03-0.20% expense ratios)
- Automatic diversification
- Tax efficient
- No stock selection required
- Historically beats most active managers

**Disadvantages:**
- You own everything (including bad companies)
- No downside protection
- You'll never beat the market (but you won't badly underperform either)

**Implementation:**
- Total market fund (VTI, VTSAX)
- S&P 500 fund (VOO, FXAIX)
- Add international (VXUS) and bonds (BND) for diversification

### Quality/Buy and Hold

**What it is:** Selecting high-quality individual stocks to own for years.

**Philosophy:** Own great businesses for the long term.

**Advantages:**
- Potential to outperform (if you select well)
- Learn about businesses deeply
- Can be more tax efficient (no fund distributions)
- Concentrated positions in best ideas

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires research and analysis
- Can underperform if selections are poor
- Less diversification
- More emotional when individual stocks decline

**Implementation:**
- 15-25 stocks across sectors
- Focus on quality metrics (ROIC, moat, management)
- Hold for 5+ years unless thesis breaks

### Core/Satellite Approach

**What it is:** Index fund core with individual stock satellites.

**Example allocation:**
- 70% index funds (core)
- 30% individual stocks (satellites)

**Advantages:**
- Best of both worlds
- Core provides diversification baseline
- Satellites allow conviction bets
- Limits damage from poor picks

## Building a Long-Term Portfolio

### Asset Allocation

**Traditional guidance by age:**
- Bonds % = Your age (or age - 10 for more aggressive)
- Stocks % = 100 - Your age (or 110 - age)

**Example (30-year-old):**
- Stocks: 70-80%
- Bonds: 20-30%

**More aggressive (long time horizon):**
- Stocks: 90%
- Bonds: 10%

### Geographic Diversification

Don't limit yourself to U.S. stocks:
- U.S.: 60-70%
- International developed: 20-25%
- Emerging markets: 10-15%

### Rebalancing

**Purpose:** Maintain target allocation as values change.

**Frequency:** Annually or when allocation drifts 5%+ from target.

**Method:**
- Sell overweight assets
- Buy underweight assets
- Or direct new contributions to underweight areas

---

## The Three-Fund Portfolio

Simplest effective portfolio:

1. **U.S. Total Stock Market:** VTI or VTSAX
2. **International Stocks:** VXUS or VTIAX
3. **U.S. Bonds:** BND or VBTLX

**Sample allocation (30-year-old):**
- 50% U.S. stocks
- 30% International stocks
- 20% Bonds

Rebalance annually. Done.

## Staying the Course

### Market Drops Are Normal

| Decline | Frequency | Average Duration |
|---------|-----------|------------------|
| 5%+ | 3x per year | Days to weeks |
| 10%+ | Once per year | Weeks to months |
| 20%+ | Every 3-4 years | Months |
| 30%+ | Every 10 years | 1-2 years |

**Key point:** Declines are temporary; not investing is permanent.

### The Cost of Missing Best Days

S&P 500 returns 1993-2022:
- Fully invested: 9.7% annually
- Missing 10 best days: 5.6% annually
- Missing 20 best days: 2.6% annually

The best days often come right after the worst days. If you panic sold, you miss the recovery.

### Dollar-Cost Averaging Through Downturns

When markets drop:
- Continue regular contributions
- You're buying more shares at lower prices
- Lowers average cost over time
- Turns downturns into opportunities

## When to Sell

### Good Reasons to Sell

1. **Thesis changed:** The reason you bought no longer holds
2. **Fundamentals deteriorated:** Moat eroded, management failed
3. **Valuation extreme:** Price far exceeds any reasonable value
4. **Better opportunity:** Higher confidence position available
5. **Rebalancing:** Maintaining target allocation

### Bad Reasons to Sell

1. **Price dropped:** If thesis intact, consider buying more
2. **Price rose:** Winners can keep winning
3. **Market fear:** Panic isn't strategy
4. **Hot tip:** Acting on noise
5. **Boredom:** Doing nothing is a valid strategy

### The Sell Discipline

Before selling, ask:
- Would I buy this stock today at this price?
- Has anything fundamental changed?
- Am I acting on emotion or analysis?
- What will I do with proceeds?

## Tax Efficiency

### Hold Period Matters

- **Under 1 year:** Short-term gains taxed as income (up to 37%)
- **Over 1 year:** Long-term gains taxed at 0-20%

Holding longer = paying less tax.

### Tax-Loss Harvesting

- Sell losers to offset gains
- Reinvest in similar (not identical) investment
- Reduces tax bill while maintaining exposure

### Asset Location

- Tax-efficient investments (stocks, index ETFs) in taxable accounts
- Tax-inefficient investments (bonds, REITs) in tax-advantaged accounts

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Checking Too Often

Daily price checking increases anxiety and trading impulses. Check quarterly at most.

### 2. Performance Chasing

Last year's best-performing fund is often next year's laggard. Stick to your plan.

### 3. Trying to Time

Missing even a few best days destroys returns. Stay invested.

### 4. Too Conservative Too Young

At 25, being 50% bonds sacrifices decades of growth. Match risk to time horizon.

### 5. Overcomplicating

Three funds can beat most complicated portfolios. Simplicity works.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Time in market > timing the market** - Stay invested through cycles
- **Index investing works** - Most investors are best served by simple index funds
- **Quality matters for stock pickers** - Focus on moats, management, financials
- **Let winners run** - Great investments drive returns
- **Don't panic sell** - Declines are temporary
- **Stay the course** - Discipline is the edge
- **Keep costs low** - Fees compound against you
