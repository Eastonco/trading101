# Chapter 23: Dollar Cost Averaging

## Overview

Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA) is the practice of investing fixed amounts at regular intervals regardless of market conditions. It removes the pressure of timing decisions and builds wealth systematically through discipline rather than prediction.

---

## How DCA Works

### The Concept

Instead of investing a lump sum all at once, you invest the same dollar amount periodically:

**Example:**
- $500 every month into an S&P 500 fund
- When prices are high, you buy fewer shares
- When prices are low, you buy more shares
- Over time, your average cost tends to be favorable

### The Math

| Month | Investment | Share Price | Shares Bought |
|-------|------------|-------------|---------------|
| 1 | $500 | $50 | 10.00 |
| 2 | $500 | $45 | 11.11 |
| 3 | $500 | $40 | 12.50 |
| 4 | $500 | $55 | 9.09 |
| 5 | $500 | $50 | 10.00 |
| **Total** | **$2,500** | **Avg: $48** | **52.70** |

Your average cost per share: $47.44 (lower than the average price of $48)

---

## Benefits of DCA

### 1. Removes Timing Pressure

- No need to predict market bottoms
- No regret from poor timing decisions
- Reduces decision fatigue

### 2. Behavioral Benefits

- Creates automatic investing habit
- Removes emotion from the process
- Prevents panic selling (you're always buying)
- Reduces temptation to time

### 3. Takes Advantage of Volatility

- Buy more shares when prices drop
- Natural "buy low" mechanism
- Turns market drops into opportunities

### 4. Accessible

- Start with any amount
- Works with automated transfers
- No large lump sum required

---

## DCA vs. Lump Sum Investing

### The Data

Studies show that **lump sum investing beats DCA about 2/3 of the time** because markets generally rise over time.

**However:**

- The difference is typically small (1-2% annually)
- DCA significantly reduces regret risk
- DCA is psychologically easier
- Many people don't have lump sums anyway

### When Lump Sum Makes Sense

- You have a windfall (inheritance, bonus)
- You have high conviction
- Long time horizon
- Can handle short-term volatility emotionally

### When DCA Makes Sense

- Regular income stream (most people)
- Nervous about market timing
- Want to reduce regret risk
- Prioritize peace of mind over optimization

### The Practical Reality

Most people invest via DCA naturally through payroll deductions to 401(k) and regular contributions to IRAs. The "lump sum vs. DCA" debate is mostly theoretical.

---

## Implementing DCA

### Automate Everything

**Setup:**
1. Set up automatic transfers from bank to brokerage
2. Schedule automatic investments into chosen funds
3. Increase contribution with each raise
4. Never look at it (mostly)

**Tools:**
- 401(k) payroll deductions (automatic)
- IRA auto-invest features
- Brokerage recurring investments
- Most brokers offer automatic investing

### Frequency

| Frequency | Pros | Cons |
|-----------|------|------|
| Weekly | More averaging | More transactions |
| Bi-weekly | Matches many pay cycles | Good balance |
| Monthly | Simple, common | Slightly less averaging |
| Quarterly | Fewer transactions | Less smoothing |

**For most people:** Monthly or bi-weekly aligns with income timing.

### Investment Choices

DCA works best with:
- Broad index funds (VTI, VOO)
- Target-date funds
- Diversified ETFs

Less ideal for:
- Individual stocks (may continue declining)
- Sector funds (concentration risk)

## DCA During Market Drops

### The Opportunity

Market drops are when DCA shines:
- Same dollars buy more shares
- You're systematically buying the dip
- No timing decision required

### Example: 2008 Financial Crisis

Investor contributing $500/month to S&P 500:

| Year | Avg S&P 500 | Shares Bought |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| 2007 | 1,478 | ~4.1 shares |
| 2008 | 1,220 | ~4.9 shares |
| 2009 | 948 | ~6.3 shares |

The 2008-2009 purchases at lower prices boosted long-term returns significantly.

### The Psychology Challenge

**When markets crash:**
- It feels wrong to keep investing
- Every instinct says "stop the bleeding"
- Media is screaming doom

**The discipline:**
- Continue contributions regardless
- These are the best buying opportunities
- Future you will thank present you

## Variations on DCA

### Value Averaging

**How it works:**
- Target a specific portfolio value growth per period
- Invest more when portfolio is below target
- Invest less (or sell) when above target

**Example:**
- Target: Portfolio grows $500/month
- If growth is $300, invest $700
- If growth is $700, invest $300

**Pros:** More systematic "buy low"
**Cons:** More complex, may require selling

### Increasing Contributions

**Annual increases:**
- Raise contribution by fixed % annually
- Match to raises (invest your raise)
- Accelerates wealth building

**Example:**
- Start at $500/month
- Increase 5% per year
- Year 10: $774/month (auto-adjusted)

## DCA and Retirement Accounts

### 401(k)

DCA happens automatically through payroll:
- Set contribution percentage
- Money deducted each paycheck
- Invested in chosen funds
- Perfect DCA implementation

### IRA

Can set up automatic:
- Monthly bank transfers
- Auto-invest in chosen funds
- Max out $7,000/year in 12 payments = $583/month

### HSA

Same approach:
- Payroll deduction or bank transfer
- Auto-invest (if your HSA allows)
- $4,150/year ÷ 12 = $346/month

## Common DCA Mistakes

### 1. Stopping During Downturns

The exact time DCA is most valuable is when it feels worst.

### 2. Not Starting

"Waiting for a pullback" means missing gains. Start now, at whatever amount.

### 3. Trying to Time Within DCA

"I'll skip this month because market seems high" defeats the purpose.

### 4. Too Conservative Allocation

DCA into bonds only provides limited benefit. Growth assets benefit most from averaging.

### 5. Not Increasing Over Time

Your contributions should grow with your income.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **DCA removes timing decisions** - Invest consistently regardless of market
- **Lump sum usually wins statistically** - But DCA wins psychologically
- **Automation is key** - Set it and forget it
- **Downturns are opportunities** - Keep investing through crashes
- **Volatility is your friend** - More volatility = more averaging benefit
- **Start now, any amount** - The habit matters more than the amount
- **Increase over time** - Grow contributions with income
