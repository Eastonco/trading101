# Chapter 50: Tax-Efficient Retirement

## Overview

Taxes don't end at retirement—they can actually become more complex. Between Social Security taxation, RMDs, and multiple account types, managing your tax burden requires strategic thinking. This chapter covers Roth conversions, bracket management, and tax-efficient legacy planning to help you keep more of what you've saved.

---

## The Retirement Tax Landscape

### Income Sources and Their Taxation

| Income Source | Tax Treatment |
|---------------|---------------|
| Social Security | 0-85% taxable (based on income) |
| Traditional IRA/401(k) withdrawals | 100% ordinary income |
| Roth IRA withdrawals | 0% (tax-free) |
| Pension | Ordinary income (some exclusions for contributions) |
| Taxable account dividends | Qualified: 0-20%; Ordinary: Ordinary rates |
| Taxable account capital gains | 0%, 15%, or 20% (long-term) |

### Why This Creates Opportunities

**You control timing.** Unlike working years when your salary is your salary, retirement lets you choose when and from which accounts to withdraw.

## Roth Conversions

### What Is a Roth Conversion?

**Moving money from a traditional IRA/401(k) to a Roth IRA,** paying taxes now for tax-free growth and withdrawals later.

### Why Convert?

**Benefits:**
- No RMDs on converted amounts (Roth IRA)
- Tax-free growth going forward
- Tax-free withdrawals
- Lower future taxable income
- Estate planning benefits

### The Golden Conversion Years

**Best time to convert:** After you stop working but before:
- Social Security starts
- RMDs begin
- Pension income starts

**These are your lowest income years** - Fill up low tax brackets.

### Conversion Strategy Example

**Married couple, age 62, just retired:**
- No income yet (before Social Security)
- Standard deduction: ~$30,000
- 10% bracket: Up to ~$23,200
- 12% bracket: Up to ~$94,300

**Strategy:**
- Convert ~$64,000 from traditional IRA
- After standard deduction: ~$34,000 taxable
- Tax: ~$3,500 (~5.5% effective rate)
- Beats 22%+ tax in RMD years

### Conversion Math

**Should you convert?**

Consider:
- Current tax rate on conversion
- Expected tax rate on future withdrawals
- Investment growth assumptions
- State tax implications
- Medicare premium impacts (IRMAA)

**General guideline:**
- Converting at 12% or lower: Usually beneficial
- Converting at 22-24%: Depends on circumstances
- Converting at higher rates: Usually wait unless estate planning

### Conversion Execution

**How to do it:**
1. Open Roth IRA (if you don't have one)
2. Request conversion from traditional IRA custodian
3. Can convert any amount (no limits)
4. Taxes due on next year's return
5. Pay taxes from outside the account (don't withhold)

**Important:** Don't withhold taxes from the conversion itself—pay separately. Withholding triggers the 10% early withdrawal penalty if under 59½.

## Tax Bracket Management

### Understanding Brackets

**2024 Tax Brackets (Married Filing Jointly):**

| Bracket | Income Range | Tax on Bracket |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| 10% | $0 - $23,200 | $2,320 |
| 12% | $23,201 - $94,300 | $8,532 |
| 22% | $94,301 - $201,050 | $23,485 |
| 24% | $201,051 - $383,900 | $43,884 |
| 32% | $383,901 - $487,450 | $33,136 |
| 35% | $487,451 - $731,200 | $85,312 |
| 37% | $731,201+ | Remaining |

### The "Fill the Bracket" Strategy

**Goal:** Each year, fill up to the top of your current bracket.

**Example:**
- Social Security: $36,000
- Taxable portion (85%): $30,600
- Traditional IRA withdrawal needed: $20,000
- Taxable income: $50,600

**12% bracket ceiling:** $94,300
**Room in bracket:** $94,300 - $50,600 = $43,700

**Strategy:** Convert additional $43,700 to Roth at 12% rate.

### Why This Matters

**Without bracket management:**
- Let traditional IRA grow
- RMDs force $100,000+ withdrawals
- Income pushes into 24%+ bracket
- Social Security 85% taxable

**With bracket management:**
- Convert in low-bracket years
- Smaller traditional IRA balance
- Lower RMDs
- More tax-free Roth income

## Social Security Taxation Strategy

### The Tax Torpedo

**Problem:** Traditional IRA withdrawals can cause MORE of your Social Security to be taxed.

**Example:**
- Social Security: $30,000
- If no other income: $0 taxable
- Add $30,000 IRA withdrawal: $25,500 SS becomes taxable
- Effective tax rate on IRA withdrawal: Much higher than bracket rate

### Managing the Torpedo

**Strategies:**
1. Spend down traditional IRA before Social Security
2. Use Roth withdrawals (don't count in combined income)
3. Delay Social Security while doing Roth conversions
4. Use taxable account funds (only gains count)

### Ideal Timeline

**Ages 62-67:** Heavy Roth conversions (no SS income)
**Ages 67-70:** Start Social Security, use Roth for spending
**Ages 70+:** Mix of SS, Roth, and minimal traditional (RMDs)

## IRMAA: Medicare Premium Surcharges

### What Is IRMAA?

**Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount:** Higher-income retirees pay more for Medicare Part B and D.

### 2024 IRMAA Brackets (Individual)

| MAGI | Part B Monthly | Part D Surcharge |
|------|----------------|------------------|
| ≤$103,000 | $174.70 | $0 |
| $103,001-129,000 | $244.60 | $12.90 |
| $129,001-161,000 | $349.40 | $33.30 |
| $161,001-193,000 | $454.20 | $53.80 |
| $193,001-500,000 | $559.00 | $74.20 |
| >$500,000 | $594.00 | $81.00 |

### Managing IRMAA

**Important:** IRMAA is based on income from 2 years prior.

**Strategies:**
- Plan large Roth conversions for years before age 63 (affects Medicare at 65)
- Spread conversions to stay below thresholds
- Consider IRMAA cost when calculating conversion benefit

**Example:**
- Large conversion at 64 affects IRMAA at 66
- $2,000 extra Medicare premiums may not be worth $5,000 tax savings

## Asset Location in Retirement

### Where to Hold What

**Tax-advantaged accounts (Traditional IRA, 401k):**
- Bonds and bond funds
- REITs
- High-dividend stocks
- Actively traded positions

**Tax-advantaged accounts (Roth IRA):**
- Highest growth potential assets
- Assets you'll hold longest
- Avoid bonds (waste of tax-free growth)

**Taxable accounts:**
- Index funds (tax-efficient)
- Stocks for long-term gains
- Municipal bonds (tax-free income)
- Tax-loss harvesting opportunities

### The Bucket Approach and Asset Location

**Bucket 1 (Cash):** Taxable or Traditional
**Bucket 2 (Bonds):** Traditional IRA (tax-inefficient anyway)
**Bucket 3 (Stocks):** Split - Taxable and Roth

## Legacy Planning

### The Stretch IRA Is Gone

**SECURE Act 2019:** Most non-spouse beneficiaries must withdraw inherited IRAs within 10 years.

**Implications:**
- Large taxable inheritance if traditional IRA
- Tax-free inheritance if Roth IRA
- Children may receive IRA during peak earning years (high tax rates)

### Roth Conversions for Legacy

**Converting traditional to Roth:**
- You pay taxes at your (probably lower) rate
- Heirs inherit tax-free
- No RMDs for you or heirs (for Roth)
- 10-year rule still applies, but no tax burden

### Leaving Different Assets

**Consider which accounts to spend vs. leave:**

| Leave to Heirs | Reason |
|----------------|--------|
| Taxable accounts | Stepped-up basis at death |
| Roth IRAs | Tax-free inheritance |
| Life insurance | Tax-free, bypasses probate |

| Spend Down | Reason |
|------------|--------|
| Traditional IRAs | Avoids high taxes for heirs |
| Assets with gains | Heirs get stepped-up basis |

### Stepped-Up Basis

**What it is:** When you die, your heirs receive taxable assets at current value, erasing capital gains.

**Example:**
- You bought stock at $10,000, now worth $100,000
- You sell: $90,000 capital gain
- Heirs inherit: Cost basis becomes $100,000
- Heirs sell immediately: $0 capital gain

**Implication:** Never sell appreciated taxable assets to fund Roth conversions if you plan to leave them to heirs.

## Tax Planning Calendar

### Annual Tax Planning Tasks

**January-February:**
- Review prior year RMD (if applicable)
- Estimate current year income

**Throughout Year:**
- Tax-loss harvest in taxable accounts
- Monitor income for bracket management
- Track qualified charitable distributions

**October-November:**
- Estimate full-year income
- Calculate remaining room in bracket
- Execute Roth conversions
- Consider tax-gain harvesting (0% bracket)

**December:**
- Final Roth conversion decisions
- RMD by December 31 (if not first year)
- Charitable giving (including QCDs)

## Working with Professionals

### When to Get Help

**Consider professional advice for:**
- Large Roth conversion decisions
- Estate planning with significant assets
- Complex income situations
- State tax implications
- Coordination with other planning

### Types of Professionals

**CPA/Tax professional:** Tax preparation, planning advice
**CFP (Certified Financial Planner):** Comprehensive financial planning
**Estate attorney:** Wills, trusts, estate documents
**Investment advisor:** Portfolio management

## Key Takeaways

- **Roth conversions are powerful** - Low-income years are golden opportunities
- **Fill up low brackets** - Don't leave 10-12% room unused
- **Social Security taxation is complex** - Roth withdrawals don't trigger SS taxes
- **IRMAA can erode conversion benefits** - Plan around thresholds
- **Asset location still matters** - Bonds in traditional, growth in Roth
- **Legacy planning favors Roth** - Pay taxes now, heirs inherit tax-free
- **Stepped-up basis is valuable** - Don't sell appreciated assets unnecessarily
- **Professional help is worthwhile** - Complex decisions have lasting impacts
