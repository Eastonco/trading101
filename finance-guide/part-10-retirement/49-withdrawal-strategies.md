# Chapter 49: Withdrawal Strategies

## Overview

Accumulating wealth for retirement is one challenge; drawing it down sustainably is another. This chapter covers how to withdraw from your retirement accounts—managing sequence of returns risk, implementing bucket strategies, and navigating Required Minimum Distributions. Getting this right can mean the difference between a comfortable retirement and running out of money.

---

## Sequence of Returns Risk

### What It Is

**Sequence of returns risk:** The order of investment returns matters when you're withdrawing money.

### Why Order Matters

**The problem:** Negative returns early in retirement hurt more than negative returns later.

### Example

**Two retirees, both average 7% annually, different sequences:**

**Retiree A (bad returns early):**

| Year | Return | Start Balance | Withdrawal | End Balance |
|------|--------|---------------|------------|-------------|
| 1 | -15% | $1,000,000 | $40,000 | $810,000 |
| 2 | -10% | $810,000 | $41,200 | $691,920 |
| 3 | +25% | $691,920 | $42,436 | $812,464 |
| 4 | +20% | $812,464 | $43,709 | $932,147 |

**After 4 years:** $932,147

**Retiree B (good returns early):**

| Year | Return | Start Balance | Withdrawal | End Balance |
|------|--------|---------------|------------|-------------|
| 1 | +20% | $1,000,000 | $40,000 | $1,152,000 |
| 2 | +25% | $1,152,000 | $41,200 | $1,398,500 |
| 3 | -10% | $1,398,500 | $42,436 | $1,216,458 |
| 4 | -15% | $1,216,458 | $43,709 | $990,437 |

**After 4 years:** $990,437

**Same average return, $58,290 difference** - And it compounds over 30 years.

### Managing Sequence Risk

**Strategies:**
1. Reduce equity allocation entering retirement
2. Maintain cash/bond buffer for withdrawals
3. Be flexible with spending
4. Consider annuitizing a portion

## The Bucket Strategy

### What It Is

**Divide your portfolio into "buckets" based on when you'll need the money.**

### Three-Bucket Approach

**Bucket 1: Short-term (1-2 years)**
- Cash, money market, short-term bonds
- Immediate spending needs
- No market risk
- Amount: 2 years of expenses

**Bucket 2: Medium-term (3-7 years)**
- Bonds, balanced funds
- Moderate growth
- Refills Bucket 1
- Amount: 5 years of expenses

**Bucket 3: Long-term (8+ years)**
- Stocks, growth investments
- Maximum growth potential
- Refills Bucket 2 over time
- Amount: Everything else

### Example ($1,500,000 portfolio, $50,000/year expenses)

| Bucket | Years | Amount | Investments |
|--------|-------|--------|-------------|
| 1 | 2 | $100,000 | Money market, CDs |
| 2 | 5 | $250,000 | Bond funds, balanced funds |
| 3 | 8+ | $1,150,000 | Stock index funds |

### How It Works

**Normal times:**
- Withdraw from Bucket 1
- Rebalance by moving from 3 → 2 → 1

**Market crash:**
- Continue withdrawing from Bucket 1
- Don't touch Bucket 3
- Bucket 3 has years to recover
- Psychological comfort: "I don't need to sell stocks"

### Bucket Strategy Benefits

- Reduces sequence of returns risk
- Provides psychological comfort
- Creates spending runway
- Forces disciplined approach

## Systematic Withdrawal Strategies

### Fixed Percentage Method

**How it works:** Withdraw fixed percentage annually (e.g., 4%).

**Pros:** Simple, adjusts somewhat to portfolio size
**Cons:** Income varies significantly with market

**Example:**
- Year 1: $1,000,000 × 4% = $40,000
- Market drops 20%: $800,000 × 4% = $32,000
- Income fell 20%

### Fixed Dollar Method (4% Rule)

**How it works:** Start with 4%, adjust for inflation annually.

**Pros:** Stable income, planning certainty
**Cons:** May deplete too fast or leave too much

### Dynamic Withdrawal Strategies

**Guardrails method:**
- Set ceiling and floor (e.g., initial withdrawal ± 20%)
- If portfolio grows, can increase spending
- If portfolio falls, must decrease spending

**Example guardrails:**
- Initial: $40,000 (4%)
- Ceiling: $48,000 (if portfolio up significantly)
- Floor: $32,000 (if portfolio down significantly)
- Adjust within these bounds

## Withdrawal Order

### The Traditional Order

**Tax-efficient withdrawal sequence:**
1. Taxable accounts first
2. Tax-deferred accounts (traditional IRA, 401k) second
3. Tax-free accounts (Roth) last

**Rationale:**
- Taxable: Selling may trigger gains, but may have stepped-up basis
- Tax-deferred: RMDs will force withdrawals anyway
- Tax-free: Let it grow tax-free as long as possible

### More Sophisticated Approach

**Fill up low tax brackets:**
- Withdraw from traditional accounts to "fill" lower brackets
- Even before you need the money
- Reduces RMDs later
- Creates tax diversification

**Example:**
- Couple's income: $30,000 (Social Security)
- 12% bracket ceiling: ~$89,450 (2024 married filing jointly)
- Gap: $59,450 of low-bracket room
- Strategy: Withdraw $59,450 from traditional IRA at 12%

### Roth Conversion Opportunity

**Early retirement (before Social Security, RMDs):**
- Income may be very low
- Convert traditional IRA to Roth
- Pay low taxes now
- Reduce future RMDs

## Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

### What Are RMDs?

**Forced withdrawals from tax-deferred accounts** beginning at age 73 (as of 2024).

### RMD Rules

**Traditional IRA, 401(k), 403(b), etc.:**
- Must begin by April 1 following year you turn 73
- Must take distribution annually thereafter
- Calculated based on IRS life expectancy tables

**Roth IRA:**
- No RMDs during owner's lifetime
- Inherited Roths have RMD rules

### Calculating RMDs

**Formula:** Account Balance (Dec 31) / Life Expectancy Factor

**Example (Age 73):**
- Account balance: $500,000
- Life expectancy factor: 26.5
- RMD: $500,000 / 26.5 = $18,868

### RMD Table (Uniform Lifetime Table)

| Age | Factor | Age | Factor |
|-----|--------|-----|--------|
| 73 | 26.5 | 80 | 20.2 |
| 74 | 25.5 | 85 | 16.0 |
| 75 | 24.6 | 90 | 12.2 |
| 76 | 23.7 | 95 | 8.9 |
| 77 | 22.9 | 100 | 6.4 |

### RMD Penalties

**Failure to take full RMD:**
- 25% penalty on amount not withdrawn
- Reduced to 10% if corrected within 2 years

### RMD Strategies

**Reduce RMDs before 73:**
- Roth conversions
- Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs) after 70.5
- Delay Social Security to do more conversions

**Qualified Charitable Distribution (QCD):**
- Donate up to $105,000 directly from IRA to charity
- Counts toward RMD
- Not included in taxable income
- Better than cash donation + deduction

## Healthcare Costs in Retirement

### Before Medicare (Pre-65)

**Options:**
- COBRA (18-36 months, expensive)
- ACA Marketplace plans
- Spouse's employer plan
- Part-time job with benefits

**Costs:** $500-2,000+/month for couple

### Medicare (65+)

**Parts:**
- Part A: Hospital (usually free)
- Part B: Medical ($174.70/month 2024, income-based)
- Part D: Prescription drugs (varies)
- Medigap/Supplement: Fills coverage gaps
- Medicare Advantage: Alternative to traditional Medicare

**Total cost estimate:** $300-600+/month per person

### Long-Term Care

**Statistics:**
- 70% of 65-year-olds will need some long-term care
- Average nursing home: $8,000-10,000+/month

**Options:**
- Self-insure (save enough)
- Long-term care insurance (expensive, complex)
- Hybrid life insurance/LTC policies

## Putting It All Together

### Sample Withdrawal Plan

**Couple retiring at 65:**
- Portfolio: $1,500,000
- Social Security (combined, starting at 67): $4,000/month
- Annual expenses: $80,000

**Years 65-67 (before Social Security):**
- Withdraw $80,000/year from portfolio
- Heavy Roth conversions while income low
- Use taxable accounts first

**Years 67-73:**
- Social Security: $48,000
- Portfolio withdrawal: $32,000
- Continue Roth conversions to fill 12% bracket
- Total: $80,000

**Years 73+:**
- Social Security: $48,000 (plus COLA increases)
- RMDs begin (may exceed $32,000 needed)
- Use QCDs for charitable giving
- Roth for discretionary spending

## Key Takeaways

- **Sequence of returns risk is real** - Bad returns early hurt more
- **Bucket strategy provides runway and comfort** - 2-7 years of bonds/cash
- **Withdrawal order matters** - Fill low brackets from traditional first
- **RMDs start at 73** - Plan to reduce account size before then
- **Roth conversions in low-income years** - Powerful strategy
- **Healthcare is expensive** - Plan for $500-2,000/month pre-Medicare
- **QCDs are tax-efficient giving** - After 70.5, give from IRA
- **Flexibility is key** - Adjust spending based on market conditions
