## Overview

## Hedging with Futures

### What Is Hedging?

**Hedging uses futures to reduce risk in an existing position or business exposure.**

This is the original purpose of futures markets—allowing producers and consumers to lock in prices.

### Portfolio Hedging Example

**Scenario:** You have $500,000 in S&P 500 index funds and are worried about a short-term decline but don't want to sell (tax reasons, long-term view).

**Hedge:**
- S&P 500 at 5,000
- 1 ES contract = $50 × 5,000 = $250,000
- Sell 2 ES contracts to hedge $500,000 portfolio

**Outcome if market falls 10%:**
- Portfolio loses $50,000
- Short futures gain $50,000
- Net: ~$0 (hedged)

**Outcome if market rises 10%:**
- Portfolio gains $50,000
- Short futures lose $50,000
- Net: ~$0 (opportunity cost)

### Commodity Producer Hedging

**Farmer with corn:**
- Harvest in 6 months
- Current price: $5.00/bushel
- Worried prices will fall

**Hedge:**
- Sell corn futures at $5.00
- At harvest, if price is $4.50:
  - Sell physical corn at $4.50
  - Futures profit: $0.50/bushel
  - Effective price: $5.00 (locked in)

## Directional Trading

### Long Futures (Bullish)

**Strategy:** Buy futures expecting price to rise.

**Example:**
- Buy 1 E-mini S&P 500 at 5,000
- Market rises to 5,100
- Profit: 100 points × $50 = $5,000

**Risk:** Market can fall indefinitely. Loss potential is substantial.

### Short Futures (Bearish)

**Strategy:** Sell futures expecting price to fall.

**Example:**
- Sell 1 crude oil futures at $80
- Oil falls to $70
- Profit: $10 × 1,000 barrels = $10,000

**Risk:** Market can rise indefinitely. Loss potential is unlimited.

### Managing Directional Trades

**Entry rules:**
- Technical analysis (support/resistance, trends)
- Fundamental catalysts
- Risk/reward assessment

**Exit rules:**
- Predefined stop-loss (essential)
- Profit targets
- Time-based exits
- Trailing stops

## Spread Trading

### What Is Spread Trading?

**Buying one futures contract and selling another related contract** to profit from the price difference (spread) changing.

### Calendar Spreads

**Same underlying, different expirations:**
- Buy June crude oil
- Sell September crude oil

**Profit from:** Change in the spread between months

**Lower risk than outright:** Both legs hedge each other
**Lower margin:** Recognized as a spread position

### Inter-Commodity Spreads

**Related but different commodities:**
- Buy gold, sell silver (gold/silver ratio)
- Buy corn, sell wheat

**Profit from:** Relative price change between commodities

### Crack Spread (Energy)

**Relationship between crude and refined products:**
- Buy crude oil
- Sell gasoline and heating oil

**Represents:** Refiner's margin

## Leverage Management

### The Leverage Problem

**Futures offer extreme leverage:**
- 10-20:1 or more
- Small moves = Large swings
- Both directions

### Responsible Leverage Use

**Calculate "effective leverage":**

Effective Leverage = (Contract Value × Contracts) / Account Value

**Example:**
- Account: $50,000
- 1 ES contract: $250,000
- Effective leverage: 5:1

**Recommended:** Keep effective leverage under 3:1

### Position Sizing for Futures

**Risk per trade:** 1-2% of account maximum

**Calculation:**
1. Determine dollar risk per trade ($50,000 × 1% = $500)
2. Determine stop-loss distance (e.g., 20 ES points = $1,000/contract)
3. Position size: $500 / $1,000 = 0.5 contracts
4. Trade 1 micro (MES) instead, risking $100 per contract

## Risk Management

### Stop-Loss Orders

**Essential for survival:**
- Predetermined exit point
- Removes emotion from decision
- Limits catastrophic losses

**Types:**
- Fixed dollar stop
- Technical stop (below support)
- Volatility-based stop (ATR multiple)

### Position Limits

**Self-imposed limits:**
- Maximum contracts per position
- Maximum positions at one time
- Maximum daily loss (stop trading after X)

### Overnight Risk

**Markets can gap:**
- Economic data releases
- Geopolitical events
- After-hours earnings

**Management:**
- Reduce position size for overnight holds
- Set "disaster stops" far from market
- Accept that stops may not fill at expected price

### Account Management

**Never fund account with:**
- Money you need
- Borrowed funds
- Retirement savings
- Emergency fund

**Starting capital:**
- Micro contracts: $5,000-10,000 minimum
- E-mini contracts: $25,000-50,000 minimum
- Buffer for drawdowns

## The Dangers of Futures Trading

### You Can Lose More Than You Deposit

**Unlike stock investing:**
- Gap through your stop
- Account goes negative
- You owe the broker money

**Real scenario:**
- Trader holds oil futures overnight
- Oil gaps down 15% on news
- Stop-loss at 5% doesn't fill until 15%
- $10,000 account loses $15,000
- Trader owes broker $5,000

### Margin Calls Force Bad Decisions

**When account falls below maintenance:**
- Must deposit funds immediately
- Or positions liquidated at market
- Often at worst possible time

### Psychological Pressure

**24-hour markets:**
- Always something happening
- Sleep disruption
- Constant vigilance impossible

**Leverage amplifies emotions:**
- Fear intensified
- Greed intensified
- Revenge trading more damaging

### The Statistics

**Studies suggest:**
- 70-80% of retail futures traders lose money
- Average retail trader underperforms significantly
- Most quit within first year
- Losses are often substantial

## Who Should Trade Futures?

### Potentially Appropriate For:

**Hedgers:**
- Businesses with commodity exposure
- Portfolio managers hedging positions
- Those with legitimate risk management needs

**Very experienced traders:**
- Multi-year trading experience
- Proven profitability in other markets
- Deep understanding of risk
- Sufficient capital to survive drawdowns

### Not Appropriate For:

**Most retail investors:**
- Insufficient capital
- Lack of experience
- Better alternatives exist (options, ETFs)
- 24/7 market is unsustainable lifestyle

**Those seeking quick profits:**
- Leverage cuts both ways
- Quick losses more likely than quick profits

## If You Still Want to Trade Futures

### Getting Started Safely

1. **Education:** 6-12 months minimum before real money
2. **Paper trade:** 3-6 months with realistic size
3. **Micro contracts only:** Start with smallest possible
4. **Strict rules:** Written trading plan before starting
5. **Capital:** Only money you can afford to lose entirely
6. **Mentorship:** Consider learning from experienced trader

### Trading Plan Requirements

**Before any trade, document:**
- Entry criteria
- Exit criteria (profit and loss)
- Position size calculation
- Maximum daily loss
- When you'll stop trading

### Red Flags You're in Trouble

- Checking prices constantly
- Sleep disruption
- Increasing position sizes after losses
- Moving stops to avoid taking losses
- Adding to losing positions
- Trading outside your plan
- Emotional attachment to positions

## Alternatives to Consider

### For Speculation

**Options:** Defined risk, similar leverage, no margin calls
**Leveraged ETFs:** 2-3x exposure, no margin account needed
**Stocks:** Lower risk, dividends, ownership

### For Hedging

**Options:** Protective puts, collars
**Inverse ETFs:** Short exposure without margin
**Reduce positions:** Sometimes simplest hedge is selling

### For Commodity Exposure

**Commodity stocks:** Producer equities
**Commodity ETFs:** Despite roll costs, simpler
**Physical metals:** For gold/silver

## Key Takeaways

- **Hedging is the legitimate use** - Businesses managing actual risk
- **Speculation requires extreme caution** - Most retail traders lose
- **Leverage is dangerous** - Can lose more than deposited
- **Margin calls force bad decisions** - Account can go negative
- **24/7 markets are exhausting** - Not sustainable for most
- **Micro contracts reduce risk** - If you must trade, start tiny
- **Better alternatives exist** - Options, ETFs often more suitable
- **Most people should avoid futures entirely** - Honest advice
