# Chapter 41: Commodities Investing

## Overview

Commodities—physical goods like gold, oil, and agricultural products—offer diversification benefits and inflation protection. Unlike stocks, which represent ownership in businesses, commodities are raw materials whose prices are driven by supply and demand fundamentals. This chapter explores how to invest in commodities and whether they belong in your portfolio.

---

## What Are Commodities?

### Definition

**Commodities are physical goods that are interchangeable with other goods of the same type.** One barrel of oil is essentially the same as another barrel of the same grade.

### Categories

**Energy:**
- Crude oil (WTI, Brent)
- Natural gas
- Gasoline
- Heating oil

**Precious Metals:**
- Gold
- Silver
- Platinum
- Palladium

**Industrial Metals:**
- Copper
- Aluminum
- Zinc
- Nickel

**Agriculture:**
- Grains (corn, wheat, soybeans)
- Softs (coffee, sugar, cocoa, cotton)
- Livestock (cattle, hogs)

---

## Why Invest in Commodities?

### Inflation Protection

**Commodities often rise with inflation:**
- Raw material costs drive inflation
- Gold historically stores value during currency debasement
- Real assets vs. paper assets

### Portfolio Diversification

**Low correlation to stocks and bonds:**
- Different price drivers
- Can perform well when stocks struggle
- Reduces overall portfolio volatility

### Supply and Demand Fundamentals

**Physical supply constraints:**
- Oil production limits
- Mine output
- Weather affecting crops
- Industrial demand cycles

### Currency Hedge

**Priced in USD:**
- Dollar weakness often lifts commodity prices
- Global demand drives pricing
- Alternative store of value (gold)

## Gold: The Classic Safe Haven

### Why Gold Is Special

**Historical significance:**
- 5,000+ years as money/store of value
- Central banks hold gold reserves
- Universal acceptance globally
- No counterparty risk (physical)

### Gold Price Drivers

**Factors affecting gold:**
- Interest rates (higher rates = opportunity cost)
- Inflation expectations
- Currency movements (especially USD)
- Geopolitical uncertainty
- Central bank buying/selling
- Investment demand (ETFs, physical)

### Ways to Invest in Gold

**Physical gold:**
- Coins (American Eagles, Krugerrands)
- Bars (various sizes)
- Pros: Tangible, no counterparty risk
- Cons: Storage, insurance, premiums, liquidity

**Gold ETFs:**
- GLD (largest, holds physical gold)
- IAU (lower expense ratio)
- Pros: Liquid, no storage concerns
- Cons: Expense ratios, trust structure

**Gold mining stocks:**
- Individual miners (Barrick, Newmont)
- Mining ETFs (GDX, GDXJ)
- Pros: Leverage to gold prices, dividends
- Cons: Company-specific risks, not pure gold exposure

### Gold Allocation Considerations

**Traditional guidance:** 5-10% of portfolio
**Purpose:** Insurance, not growth engine
**Expectation:** Preserve value, not generate income

## Oil and Energy

### Oil Market Basics

**Benchmark grades:**
- WTI (West Texas Intermediate): US benchmark
- Brent: International benchmark
- Prices can differ based on quality and location

### Oil Price Drivers

**Supply factors:**
- OPEC production decisions
- US shale production
- Geopolitical disruptions
- Inventory levels

**Demand factors:**
- Global economic growth
- Transportation demand
- Industrial activity
- Weather (heating/cooling)

### Ways to Invest in Oil

**Futures (not recommended for most):**
- Direct exposure but roll costs
- Leverage and margin requirements
- Contango/backwardation issues

**Oil ETFs:**
- USO (holds front-month futures)
- Warning: Severe contango drag
- Long-term underperformance vs. spot oil

**Energy stocks:**
- Integrated majors (Exxon, Chevron)
- Exploration & Production (EOG, Pioneer)
- Oil services (Schlumberger, Halliburton)
- Pros: Dividends, operating leverage
- Cons: Company-specific risks

### The Oil ETF Problem

**USO and similar products suffer from "roll yield":**
- Futures expire monthly
- Must sell expiring contract, buy next month
- In contango (future price > spot), this costs money
- Long-term returns can dramatically lag spot oil

**2020 Example:**
- Oil "crashed" but recovered to prior levels
- USO never recovered (down 70%+ even when oil recovered)

## Commodity Investment Methods Compared

### Direct Futures

**Pros:**
- Purest exposure
- Leverage (can be pro or con)
- 24-hour markets

**Cons:**
- Roll costs (contango)
- Margin requirements
- Complexity
- Not suitable for most retail investors

### Commodity ETFs

**Pros:**
- Easy access
- No margin required
- Trade like stocks

**Cons:**
- Roll costs erode returns
- Tracking error
- Expense ratios
- Poor long-term performance vs. spot

### Producer Stocks

**Pros:**
- Operating leverage to commodity prices
- Dividends possible
- Management can add value
- Avoid roll costs

**Cons:**
- Company-specific risks
- Not pure commodity exposure
- Correlation isn't perfect
- Equity market risk

### Physical Ownership

**Pros:**
- No counterparty risk
- No tracking error
- Tangible asset

**Cons:**
- Storage costs
- Insurance
- Liquidity challenges
- Only practical for precious metals

## Contango and Backwardation

### Contango

**Definition:** Future prices > Spot prices

**Normal for:** Oil, natural gas, most commodities
**Cause:** Storage costs, financing costs, convenience yield

**Impact on investors:**
- Roll yield is negative
- ETFs lose value over time
- Even if spot price unchanged, ETF can decline

### Backwardation

**Definition:** Future prices < Spot prices

**Occurs when:** Immediate supply shortage
**Cause:** High demand for immediate delivery

**Impact on investors:**
- Roll yield is positive
- Helps ETF performance
- Less common state

### Contango Example

| Month | Futures Price |
|-------|---------------|
| Current (spot) | $70 |
| 1 month | $71 |
| 2 months | $72 |
| 3 months | $73 |

**ETF rolls from $71 to $72:**
- Sells 1 contract at $71
- Buys 1 contract at $72
- Loses $1 in the process
- Repeat monthly = significant drag

## Building a Commodity Allocation

### How Much?

**Traditional guidance:**
- 5-10% of portfolio maximum
- Diversification benefit, not growth driver
- Gold may warrant separate allocation within this

### Implementation Options

**Simple approach:**
- 5% in gold ETF (GLD or IAU)
- Avoid other commodity ETFs due to roll costs

**If you want broader commodity exposure:**
- Energy stocks instead of oil ETFs
- Mining stocks instead of metal ETFs
- Agricultural stocks instead of grain ETFs

### Rebalancing

**Commodities are volatile:**
- Can drift significantly from target
- Rebalance annually or at 5% drift
- Creates tax events in taxable accounts

## Commodity Risks

### Volatility

**Commodities are volatile:**
- Oil: 50%+ swings are common
- Gold: 30-40% drawdowns occur
- Agriculture: Weather-driven spikes and crashes

### No Income

**Commodities generate no yield:**
- No dividends
- No interest
- Return comes only from price appreciation
- Storage costs are a drag

### Roll Costs

**For ETF investors:**
- Contango erodes returns
- Can be 5-15% annually
- Long-term performance lags spot prices

### Geopolitical Risk

**Supply disruptions:**
- OPEC production cuts
- Sanctions (Russia oil)
- Trade wars (agriculture)
- Weather events

### Currency Risk

**Priced in USD:**
- Strong dollar hurts commodity prices
- Weak dollar helps commodity prices
- Unintended currency exposure

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Commodities offer diversification and inflation protection** - But not guaranteed
- **Gold is the most accessible** - Physical, ETFs, or mining stocks
- **Commodity ETFs suffer from roll costs** - Long-term performance issues
- **Producer stocks often better than ETFs** - For commodity exposure
- **Contango is a silent killer** - For most commodity ETFs
- **Keep allocations modest** - 5-10% of portfolio maximum
- **No income generation** - Pure price appreciation play
- **Volatility is significant** - Size positions accordingly
