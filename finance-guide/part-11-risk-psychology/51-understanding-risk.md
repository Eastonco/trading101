# Chapter 51: Understanding Risk

## Overview

Risk is the possibility that your actual returns differ from your expected returns. It's the price of admission for potential gains. Understanding the different types of risk, measuring your capacity and tolerance for risk, and building portfolios that match your risk profile is fundamental to investment success. This chapter lays the groundwork for the entire Risk & Psychology section.

---

## Types of Investment Risk

### Market Risk (Systematic Risk)

**What it is:** The risk that affects the entire market.

**Examples:**
- Economic recession
- Interest rate changes
- Inflation
- Global pandemics
- Geopolitical events

**Key point:** Cannot be diversified away. If you own stocks, you have market risk.

### Company-Specific Risk (Unsystematic Risk)

**What it is:** Risk specific to an individual company.

**Examples:**
- Poor management decisions
- Product failures
- Accounting scandals
- Loss of key customers
- Competitive disruption

**Key point:** CAN be diversified away. Own enough stocks and individual failures matter less.

### Sector Risk

**What it is:** Risk affecting an entire industry.

**Examples:**
- Oil price crash (energy sector)
- Regulatory changes (healthcare)
- Technology disruption (retail)
- Interest rate changes (financials, real estate)

### Interest Rate Risk

**What it is:** Risk that changes in interest rates affect investment values.

**Primarily affects:**
- Bonds (prices fall when rates rise)
- Real estate (financing costs)
- Dividend stocks (compete with bonds for income)
- Growth stocks (future earnings worth less)

### Inflation Risk

**What it is:** Risk that inflation erodes purchasing power.

**Affects:**
- Fixed-income investments (bonds with fixed coupons)
- Cash holdings
- Any asset with fixed returns

**Protection:** Stocks, TIPS, I-Bonds, real estate

### Liquidity Risk

**What it is:** Risk that you can't sell an investment quickly at fair value.

**Illiquid investments:**
- Real estate
- Private equity
- Small-cap stocks
- Some bonds
- Collectibles

### Concentration Risk

**What it is:** Risk from having too much in one investment.

**Examples:**
- Company stock from employer
- Single rental property
- Large position in one stock
- Heavy allocation to one sector

### Currency Risk

**What it is:** Risk that exchange rate changes affect returns.

**Affects:**
- International investments
- Multinational companies
- Commodities priced in USD

### Longevity Risk

**What it is:** Risk of outliving your money.

**Management:**
- Adequate savings
- Sustainable withdrawal rates
- Annuities (transfer risk to insurer)
- Social Security optimization

## Measuring Risk

### Standard Deviation (Volatility)

**What it measures:** How much returns vary from the average.

**Higher standard deviation = More volatile = More "risky" (by this measure)**

| Asset Class | Typical Std Dev |
|-------------|-----------------|
| T-Bills | 1-2% |
| Bonds (aggregate) | 4-6% |
| Large-cap stocks | 15-20% |
| Small-cap stocks | 20-25% |
| Emerging markets | 25-30% |

### Maximum Drawdown

**What it measures:** The largest peak-to-trough decline.

**Examples:**
- S&P 500 in 2008-2009: -57%
- Nasdaq 2000-2002: -78%
- Bitcoin 2021-2022: -77%

**Why it matters:** Can you hold through a 50% decline?

### Beta

**What it measures:** Volatility relative to the market.

| Beta | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 1.0 | Moves with market |
| 1.5 | 50% more volatile than market |
| 0.5 | 50% less volatile than market |
| -0.5 | Moves opposite to market |

### Sharpe Ratio

**What it measures:** Risk-adjusted return.

**Formula:** (Return - Risk-Free Rate) / Standard Deviation

**Higher = Better** - More return per unit of risk.

## Risk Tolerance vs. Risk Capacity

### Risk Tolerance

**Definition:** Your emotional ability to handle volatility.

**Determined by:**
- Personality
- Experience
- Emotions about money
- Sleep-at-night factor

**Question:** If your portfolio dropped 30%, would you:
a) Panic and sell everything
b) Lose sleep but hold
c) See it as a buying opportunity
d) Not even notice

### Risk Capacity

**Definition:** Your financial ability to take risk.

**Determined by:**
- Time horizon
- Income stability
- Savings rate
- Overall financial situation
- Other resources

**Example:**
- 25-year-old with 40-year horizon: HIGH capacity
- 65-year-old retiring next year: LOW capacity

### When They Conflict

**High tolerance, low capacity:**
- Want to be aggressive but can't afford losses
- **Follow capacity** - Reduce risk

**Low tolerance, high capacity:**
- Could afford risk but can't stomach it
- **Balance both** - Be more conservative than pure math suggests, but recognize you may undersave

### Assessing Your Risk Profile

**Questions to consider:**

1. When do you need this money?
2. How stable is your income?
3. What other financial resources exist?
4. Have you experienced a major market decline?
5. How did you feel/act during past volatility?
6. What's your actual goal for this money?

## Risk and Return

### The Fundamental Trade-Off

**Higher expected returns require accepting higher risk.**

| Asset Class | Historical Return | Standard Deviation |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| T-Bills | 3-4% | 1-2% |
| Bonds | 5-6% | 4-6% |
| Large-cap stocks | 9-10% | 15-20% |
| Small-cap stocks | 11-12% | 20-25% |

### Risk Premium

**The extra return for taking risk.**

**Equity risk premium:** ~4-6% extra return for stocks vs. bonds (historically)

### No Free Lunch

**If something offers high returns with "low risk," something is wrong:**
- Hidden risks you don't understand
- Fraud
- Survivorship bias
- Too good to be true = probably isn't true

## Risk at Different Life Stages

### Accumulation (20s-40s)

**Risk capacity:** High (long time horizon)
**Recommended:** Higher stock allocation, accept volatility
**Recovery time:** Decades to recover from any decline
**Focus:** Growth

### Mid-Career (40s-50s)

**Risk capacity:** Moderate
**Recommended:** Begin gradual de-risking
**Focus:** Growth with preservation consideration

### Pre-Retirement (5-10 Years Before)

**Risk capacity:** Declining
**Recommended:** Sequence of returns risk management
**Focus:** Preservation while maintaining growth

### Retirement

**Risk capacity:** Lower (but varies)
**Recommended:** Match allocation to withdrawal timeline
**Focus:** Income, preservation, and maintaining purchasing power

## Common Risk Mistakes

### 1. Not Taking Enough Risk

**The risk of being too safe:**
- Inflation erodes purchasing power
- May not reach retirement goals
- Working longer than necessary

**$10,000 over 30 years:**
- At 2% (safe): $18,114
- At 7% (balanced): $76,123
- Difference: $58,000

### 2. Taking Too Much Risk

**Signs you're overexposed:**
- Losing sleep over investments
- Checking portfolio obsessively
- Taking risks you don't understand
- Concentrated positions

### 3. Not Understanding the Risks

**Common blind spots:**
- Illiquidity risk in alternatives
- Leverage in ETFs
- Counterparty risk
- Sequence of returns risk

### 4. Ignoring Tail Risk

**Tail risk:** Rare but severe events

**Examples:**
- 2008 financial crisis
- March 2020 COVID crash
- Single stock blowups (Enron, FTX)

**These events happen more often than normal distributions suggest.**

### 5. Assuming Past = Future

**Historical returns don't guarantee future returns.**
**Past volatility doesn't capture future risks.**

## Risk Management Framework

### Step 1: Identify Risks

What could go wrong?
- Market decline
- Inflation
- Job loss
- Health emergency
- Specific investment failures

### Step 2: Assess Impact

If it happens, how bad?
- Catastrophic (lose everything)
- Severe (major setback)
- Moderate (uncomfortable but recoverable)
- Minor (inconvenient)

### Step 3: Assess Probability

How likely?
- Very likely (will happen)
- Likely (probably will happen)
- Possible (could happen)
- Unlikely (probably won't)
- Very unlikely (rare)

### Step 4: Manage the Risk

**Options:**
- **Accept:** Acknowledge and do nothing (minor risks)
- **Reduce:** Lower position size, diversify
- **Transfer:** Insurance, hedging
- **Avoid:** Don't take the risk at all

### Step 5: Monitor

Risks change over time. Review regularly.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Risk is multidimensional** - Market, company, sector, liquidity, inflation, and more
- **Risk tolerance vs. capacity** - Emotional vs. financial ability to take risk
- **Higher returns require higher risk** - No exceptions
- **Diversification reduces some risks** - But not market risk
- **Time horizon affects capacity** - Longer = more risk capacity
- **Not taking enough risk is itself a risk** - Inflation erodes safe investments
- **Understand what you own** - Hidden risks can surprise you
- **Monitor and adjust** - Your risk profile changes over time
