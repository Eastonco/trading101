# Chapter 52: Position Sizing & Money Management

## Overview

Position sizing is arguably the most important skill in trading and investing. It determines how much money you put into each investment—and by extension, how much you can lose. Poor position sizing destroys more accounts than bad stock picks. This chapter provides practical frameworks for sizing positions appropriately across different strategies.

---

## Why Position Sizing Matters

### The Math of Ruin

**The problem with large losses:**

| Loss | Gain Needed to Recover |
|------|------------------------|
| 10% | 11% |
| 20% | 25% |
| 30% | 43% |
| 40% | 67% |
| 50% | 100% |
| 60% | 150% |
| 70% | 233% |
| 80% | 400% |
| 90% | 900% |

**A 50% loss requires a 100% gain to recover.**

### Why Traders Blow Up

**Common scenario:**
1. Trader has successful streak
2. Confidence increases
3. Position sizes increase
4. One bad trade wipes out gains (and more)
5. Emotional decisions follow
6. Account devastated

**The fix:** Systematic position sizing that limits any single loss.

## The Percentage Risk Model

### The Core Rule

**Never risk more than 1-2% of your account on a single trade.**

### How It Works

**Formula:**
Position Size = (Account Value × Risk %) / (Entry Price - Stop Price)

### Example

**Account:** $50,000
**Risk per trade:** 1% = $500
**Stock entry:** $50
**Stop-loss:** $47 (risk $3 per share)

**Position size:** $500 / $3 = 166 shares
**Position value:** 166 × $50 = $8,300

### Why 1-2%?

**At 1% risk per trade:**
- 10 consecutive losers = 10% drawdown
- Survivable, room to recover
- Psychology manageable

**At 5% risk per trade:**
- 10 consecutive losers = 50% drawdown
- Recovery requires 100% gain
- Emotionally devastating

### Adjusting Risk Percentage

| Situation | Risk % |
|-----------|--------|
| New strategy/trader | 0.5% |
| Proven strategy | 1% |
| High conviction | 1.5% |
| Maximum ever | 2% |

## Position Sizing for Investors

### Total Position Limits

**Individual stock positions:**
- Conservative: Max 3-5% per stock
- Moderate: Max 5-7% per stock
- Aggressive: Max 8-10% per stock
- Never: Over 10% in a single stock

### Sector Limits

**Avoid sector concentration:**
- Max 20-25% in any single sector
- Technology dominance is a risk
- Financial crisis showed danger of overweight financials

### Asset Class Allocation

**Match to risk profile:**

| Profile | Stocks | Bonds | Alternatives |
|---------|--------|-------|--------------|
| Conservative | 30-40% | 50-60% | 0-10% |
| Moderate | 50-60% | 30-40% | 0-10% |
| Aggressive | 70-80% | 15-25% | 0-10% |
| Very Aggressive | 85-95% | 0-10% | 0-10% |

## The Kelly Criterion

### What It Is

**A mathematical formula for optimal bet sizing** that maximizes long-term growth.

**Formula:**
Kelly % = Win Rate - (Loss Rate / Win-Loss Ratio)

### Example Calculation

**Trading system:**
- Win rate: 55%
- Average winner: $2
- Average loser: $1
- Win/Loss ratio: 2

**Kelly %:** 0.55 - (0.45 / 2) = 0.55 - 0.225 = 32.5%

### Why Not to Use Full Kelly

**Problems with full Kelly:**
- Assumes perfect knowledge of probabilities
- Maximizes growth but also maximizes volatility
- One wrong assumption = disaster
- Psychologically difficult

### Fractional Kelly

**Better approach:** Use 1/4 to 1/2 Kelly.

**In above example:**
- Full Kelly: 32.5%
- Half Kelly: 16.25%
- Quarter Kelly: 8.1%

**Quarter Kelly provides:**
- 75% of the growth rate
- 1/16th of the volatility
- Much more survivable

## Scaling In and Out

### Scaling In

**Building a position over time rather than all at once.**

**Methods:**
- Fixed amounts at regular intervals
- Add on pullbacks
- Add as position proves correct

**Example scaling in:**
- Target: 4% position
- Initial: 1.5% position
- If up 5%: Add 1.5%
- If up 10%: Add final 1%

**Benefits:**
- Reduces timing risk
- Confirms thesis before full commitment
- Averages in at different prices

### Scaling Out

**Taking profits incrementally rather than all at once.**

**Example scaling out:**
- Sell 1/3 at first target (lock in some profit)
- Sell 1/3 at second target
- Let 1/3 ride with trailing stop

**Benefits:**
- Locks in profits
- Reduces regret (kept some)
- Allows participation in bigger moves

## Position Sizing by Strategy

### Long-Term Investing

**Typical position sizes:**
- Core holdings: 5-10%
- Satellite positions: 2-5%
- Speculative: 1-2%

**Example $100,000 portfolio:**
- VTI (Total Market): 40%
- VXUS (International): 20%
- BND (Bonds): 20%
- Individual stocks (5 positions): 4% each (20%)

### Swing Trading

**Risk-based sizing:**
- 1-2% risk per trade
- Typically 3-6 concurrent positions
- Total risk: 3-12% if all hit stops

**Example:**
- $50,000 account
- 1% risk per trade = $500
- 5 positions, each risks $500
- Maximum drawdown if all fail: 5%

### Options Trading

**Premium-based sizing:**
- Long options: 1-3% of account per position
- Credit spreads: 2-5% of account (based on max loss)
- Never more than 5% at risk in single trade

### Day Trading

**Even more conservative:**
- 0.5-1% risk per trade
- Daily loss limit: 2-3%
- Stop trading after limit hit

## Risk/Reward Ratio

### The Concept

**Risk/Reward Ratio:** How much you could lose vs. how much you could gain.

**Example:**
- Entry: $50
- Stop: $47 (risk $3)
- Target: $58 (reward $8)
- Risk/Reward: 1:2.67

### Minimum Standards

**Generally acceptable:**
- Day trading: 1:1.5 minimum
- Swing trading: 1:2 minimum
- Position trading: 1:3 minimum

### Why It Matters

**Trading with 1:2 risk/reward:**

| Win Rate | Expectancy |
|----------|------------|
| 40% | +0.20 (profitable) |
| 50% | +0.50 (very profitable) |
| 60% | +0.80 (excellent) |

**Even 40% win rate is profitable with good risk/reward.**

### Position Sizing Based on Risk/Reward

**Better risk/reward = Can take slightly larger position**

| Risk/Reward | Suggested Risk % |
|-------------|------------------|
| 1:1 | 0.5% |
| 1:2 | 1% |
| 1:3 | 1-1.5% |
| 1:4+ | 1.5-2% |

## Money Management Rules

### The 2% Rule

**Never risk more than 2% of account on any single trade.**

### The 6% Rule

**If account drops 6% in a month, stop trading and reassess.**

**Reasoning:**
- 6% loss = Something is wrong
- Could be strategy, market, or psychology
- Step back before more damage

### The Correlation Rule

**Don't count positions that move together as separate.**

**Example:**
- Long 3 bank stocks = 1 position (correlated)
- Long QQQ and 5 tech stocks = Overlapping risk

### The Leverage Rule

**If using leverage, calculate risk on full notional value.**

**Example:**
- $50,000 account
- 2:1 leverage on $20,000 position = $40,000 exposure
- 10% drop = $4,000 loss = 8% of account

## Common Position Sizing Mistakes

### 1. Sizing Based on Desired Profit

**Wrong:** "I want to make $5,000, so I'll buy 500 shares"
**Right:** "My max loss is $500, so I can buy 166 shares"

### 2. Averaging Down Without Plan

**Dangerous:** Adding to losers hoping for recovery
**Better:** Predetermined scale-in points with maximum loss

### 3. Ignoring Correlation

**Thinking:** "I'm diversified with 10 positions"
**Reality:** All 10 are tech stocks that move together

### 4. Emotional Position Sizing

**After wins:** Increasing size due to overconfidence
**After losses:** Either too aggressive (revenge) or too timid (fear)

**Solution:** Systematic sizing regardless of recent results

### 5. Not Accounting for Slippage

**Plan for:**
- Bid-ask spreads
- Gap risk
- Execution differences

**Size slightly smaller than maximum to allow for slippage.**

## Position Sizing Checklist

**Before every trade:**

- [ ] What is my maximum dollar loss?
- [ ] Is it under 1-2% of account?
- [ ] Where is my stop-loss?
- [ ] Is my risk/reward at least 1:2?
- [ ] How does this correlate with other positions?
- [ ] What's my total portfolio risk if all stops hit?
- [ ] Am I sizing based on risk or emotion?

## Key Takeaways

- **Never risk more than 1-2% per trade** - Survival first
- **Large losses are mathematically devastating** - 50% down = 100% gain to recover
- **Calculate position size from risk, not from potential profit**
- **Kelly Criterion is useful but use fractional Kelly** - 1/4 to 1/2 for safety
- **Scaling in/out reduces timing risk** - Don't commit all at once
- **Correlation matters** - Similar positions = concentrated risk
- **Risk/reward determines minimum** - Don't trade poor setups
- **Systematic sizing beats emotional sizing** - Every time
