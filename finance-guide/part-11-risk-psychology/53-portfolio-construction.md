# Chapter 53: Portfolio Construction

## Overview

Portfolio construction is the art and science of combining investments to achieve your goals while managing risk. It's not about picking the best individual investments—it's about how they work together. A well-constructed portfolio can deliver better risk-adjusted returns than its individual components. This chapter covers diversification, correlation, asset allocation, and rebalancing.

---

## The Goal of Portfolio Construction

### What We're Trying to Achieve

**Optimize the relationship between:**
- Expected return
- Risk (volatility)
- Correlation between assets

**The ideal:** Maximum return for a given level of risk, or minimum risk for a given return.

### Why the Whole Can Be Better Than Parts

**Diversification benefit:**
- Assets don't move perfectly together
- When one zigs, another zags
- Portfolio volatility < Average asset volatility

---

## Diversification

### What Diversification Is

**Owning different investments so that poor performance in one doesn't devastate your portfolio.**

### What Diversification Isn't

- Owning 20 tech stocks (concentrated, correlated)
- Owning 10 similar funds (redundant, overlap)
- Owning everything (diluted, no conviction)

### Diversification Dimensions

**Diversify across:**
- Asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate)
- Geography (domestic, international, emerging)
- Sectors (tech, healthcare, financials, etc.)
- Company size (large, mid, small cap)
- Investment style (growth, value, blend)
- Time (dollar-cost averaging)

### How Many Stocks for Diversification?

**Academic research suggests:**
- 15-20 stocks eliminates most company-specific risk
- Beyond 30-40, marginal benefit diminishes
- Index funds provide instant diversification

**Company-specific risk reduction:**

| Number of Stocks | Risk Remaining |
|------------------|----------------|
| 1 | 100% (all company risk) |
| 5 | ~65% |
| 10 | ~50% |
| 20 | ~40% |
| 30 | ~35% |
| 50 | ~30% |
| Market | ~30% (market risk only) |

## Correlation

### What Is Correlation?

**Correlation measures how two assets move relative to each other.**

**Range:** -1 to +1

| Correlation | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| +1.0 | Perfect positive (move together) |
| +0.5 | Moderate positive |
| 0 | No relationship |
| -0.5 | Moderate negative (move opposite) |
| -1.0 | Perfect negative |

### Why Low Correlation Matters

**Portfolio math:**
- Two assets with 10% expected volatility each
- If correlation = +1: Portfolio volatility = 10%
- If correlation = 0: Portfolio volatility = 7.1%
- If correlation = -1: Portfolio volatility = 0%

**Lower correlation = Lower portfolio volatility for same expected return.**

### Historical Correlations (Approximate)

| Assets | Correlation |
|--------|-------------|
| US Large Cap vs. US Small Cap | +0.80 |
| US Stocks vs. International Developed | +0.75 |
| US Stocks vs. Emerging Markets | +0.65 |
| US Stocks vs. US Bonds | +0.05 |
| US Stocks vs. Gold | +0.05 |
| US Stocks vs. REITs | +0.60 |
| Bonds vs. Gold | +0.20 |

### Correlation Isn't Constant

**In crises, correlations increase:**
- "Everything goes down together"
- Diversification benefits diminish when you need them most
- Plan for this: Don't rely solely on correlation

## Asset Allocation

### Strategic Asset Allocation

**Your long-term target allocation based on goals, timeline, and risk tolerance.**

**Example targets:**

| Profile | Stocks | Bonds | Other |
|---------|--------|-------|-------|
| Aggressive | 90% | 10% | 0% |
| Growth | 75% | 20% | 5% |
| Balanced | 60% | 35% | 5% |
| Conservative | 40% | 50% | 10% |
| Income | 25% | 65% | 10% |

### The Classic 60/40 Portfolio

**60% stocks, 40% bonds**

**Historical performance:**
- Lower returns than 100% stocks
- Significantly lower volatility
- Better risk-adjusted returns
- Smaller drawdowns

**2022 challenged this:** Both stocks and bonds fell together (unusual).

### Age-Based Allocation

**Traditional rule:** 100 - Age = Stock %

**Modern rule:** 110 or 120 - Age = Stock % (longer lifespans)

| Age | Traditional | Modern |
|-----|-------------|--------|
| 30 | 70% stocks | 80-90% stocks |
| 40 | 60% stocks | 70-80% stocks |
| 50 | 50% stocks | 60-70% stocks |
| 60 | 40% stocks | 50-60% stocks |
| 70 | 30% stocks | 40-50% stocks |

### Asset Allocation Drives Most Returns

**Studies suggest:**
- Asset allocation explains 90%+ of portfolio return variation
- Individual security selection matters less
- Market timing matters least

**Implication:** Get allocation right first.

## Building a Portfolio

### Step 1: Determine Asset Allocation

Based on:
- Time horizon
- Risk tolerance
- Risk capacity
- Goals

### Step 2: Choose Sub-Allocations

**Within stocks:**
- US vs. International
- Large vs. Small
- Growth vs. Value

**Within bonds:**
- Government vs. Corporate
- Short vs. Long duration
- US vs. International

### Step 3: Select Investments

**For each allocation:**
- Index funds (most efficient for most people)
- ETFs or mutual funds
- Individual securities (if you have time/expertise)

### Step 4: Implement

- Open appropriate accounts
- Consider tax location (where to hold what)
- Execute purchases

### Step 5: Monitor and Rebalance

- Track allocation drift
- Rebalance when needed
- Review periodically

## Rebalancing

### What Is Rebalancing?

**Returning your portfolio to target allocation** after market movements cause drift.

### Why Rebalance?

**Example:**
- Start: 60/40 stocks/bonds
- After bull market: 70/30 stocks/bonds
- Risk is now higher than intended
- Rebalancing sells high (stocks), buys low (bonds)

### Rebalancing Methods

**Calendar rebalancing:**
- Rebalance on fixed schedule (annually, quarterly)
- Simple, predictable
- May rebalance when not needed

**Threshold rebalancing:**
- Rebalance when allocation drifts 5% from target
- Only acts when needed
- Requires monitoring

**Combination:**
- Check quarterly
- Rebalance only if 5%+ drift

### Rebalancing Considerations

**Tax implications:**
- Rebalancing in taxable accounts creates taxable events
- Use new contributions to rebalance first
- Rebalance in tax-advantaged accounts when possible

**Costs:**
- Transaction fees (minimal now with most brokers)
- Bid-ask spreads

## Tax-Efficient Asset Location

### The Concept

**Hold tax-inefficient investments in tax-advantaged accounts.**

### What Goes Where

| Account Type | Best Holdings |
|--------------|---------------|
| Traditional IRA/401k | Bonds, REITs, high-dividend stocks |
| Roth IRA | Highest growth potential (stocks) |
| Taxable | Index funds, growth stocks, munis |

### Why This Matters

**Example:**
- Bond fund yields 4%, taxed at 24% = 3.04% after tax
- Same fund in IRA: Full 4% (tax-deferred)
- Stock fund with 1% dividend, 7% appreciation
- In taxable: Low tax drag (qualified dividends, long-term gains)

## Common Portfolio Mistakes

### 1. Over-Diversification (Diworsification)

**Problem:** Owning so many funds that they overlap significantly
**Solution:** Check underlying holdings; fewer funds can provide better diversification

### 2. Home Country Bias

**Problem:** Over-allocating to your home country
**Reality:** US is ~60% of world market; many investors hold 90%+
**Solution:** Include international exposure

### 3. Chasing Performance

**Problem:** Buying last year's winners
**Reality:** Past performance doesn't predict future
**Solution:** Stick to allocation strategy

### 4. Ignoring Costs

**Problem:** High expense ratios compound against you
**Solution:** Prefer low-cost index funds

### 5. Not Rebalancing

**Problem:** Portfolio drifts from target
**Solution:** Schedule regular reviews and rebalancing

### 6. Letting Emotions Drive Allocation

**Problem:** Shifting allocation based on fear/greed
**Solution:** Write down your strategy; follow it

## Key Takeaways

- **Diversification reduces risk** - Don't put all eggs in one basket
- **Correlation matters** - Combine assets that don't move together
- **Asset allocation drives returns** - More important than stock picking
- **Keep it simple** - Three funds can beat complex portfolios
- **Rebalance periodically** - Maintains target risk level
- **Consider tax location** - Put tax-inefficient assets in tax-advantaged accounts
- **Avoid common mistakes** - Over-diversification, home bias, performance chasing
- **Stick to your plan** - Changing strategy frequently hurts returns
