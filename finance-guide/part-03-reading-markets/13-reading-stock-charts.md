# Chapter 13: How to Read Stock Charts

## Overview

Stock charts are visual representations of price movement over time. Learning to read them is foundational for any investor or trader who wants to make informed decisions about when to buy, sell, or hold positions.

This chapter covers the basics of chart reading—different chart types, timeframes, and the essential elements that every chart displays.

---

## Why Charts Matter

### What Charts Tell You

A stock chart shows you:
- **Price history** - Where the stock has been
- **Trends** - Direction of movement
- **Momentum** - Speed and strength of moves
- **Support/resistance** - Key price levels
- **Volume** - Trading activity at different prices

### Two Schools of Thought

**Fundamental analysis:** Focus on company financials, valuation, business quality
**Technical analysis:** Focus on price patterns, charts, indicators

Most successful investors use both. Even fundamental investors benefit from understanding charts for entry/exit timing.

## Chart Types

### Line Chart

**What it shows:** A line connecting closing prices over time.

**Pros:**
- Clean, easy to read
- Good for seeing overall trend
- Filters out intraday noise

**Cons:**
- Loses information (no high/low/open)
- Can't see intraday volatility

**Best for:** Quick overview of long-term trends

### Bar Chart (OHLC)

**What it shows:** Each bar represents one time period with:
- **O**pen - Small left tick
- **H**igh - Top of bar
- **L**ow - Bottom of bar
- **C**lose - Small right tick

**Reading the bar:**
```
      High ─┬─
           │
   Open  ─┤│
           │
           │├─ Close
           │
      Low ─┴─
```

**Pros:**
- Shows full price range
- Visualizes volatility
- Shows relationship between open and close

**Best for:** Traditional technical analysis

### Candlestick Chart

**What it shows:** Same OHLC data as bar charts, but with filled "bodies."

**Components:**
- **Body:** Rectangle between open and close
- **Wicks/Shadows:** Lines extending to high and low
- **Color:** Green/white (bullish, close > open) or Red/black (bearish, close < open)

```
Bullish Candle    Bearish Candle
     │                  │
     │ High             │ High
     │                  │
   ┌─┴─┐ Close       ┌─┴─┐ Open
   │   │             │███│
   │   │             │███│
   └─┬─┘ Open        └─┬─┘ Close
     │                  │
     │ Low              │ Low
     │                  │
```

**Pros:**
- Visually intuitive (green = up, red = down)
- Easy to spot patterns
- Shows battle between buyers and sellers

**Best for:** Most traders prefer candlesticks; industry standard

## Timeframes

Charts can display any timeframe. Common options:

### Intraday (Day Trading)

- **1-minute:** Very short-term scalping
- **5-minute:** Day trading
- **15-minute:** Day trading
- **1-hour:** Intraday swing

### Short-Term

- **4-hour:** Short-term swing trading
- **Daily:** Most common for swing trading and position trading

### Long-Term

- **Weekly:** Position trading, investment timing
- **Monthly:** Long-term investing, major trends

### Which Timeframe to Use?

| Trading Style | Primary Timeframe | Secondary |
|---------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Day trading | 5-15 min | 1-hour |
| Swing trading | Daily | 4-hour, Weekly |
| Position trading | Weekly | Daily, Monthly |
| Long-term investing | Monthly | Weekly |

**Multi-timeframe analysis:** Look at multiple timeframes for context. The daily trend within the weekly trend within the monthly trend.

## Essential Chart Elements

### Price Scale (Y-Axis)

**Linear scale:** Equal dollar amounts have equal distance
- $10 to $20 = same distance as $100 to $110

**Logarithmic scale:** Equal percentage moves have equal distance
- 10% move always same distance regardless of price
- Better for long-term charts of stocks with large price changes

**Use logarithmic for:** Stocks that have moved significantly over time (e.g., viewing 10+ years of data)

### Time Scale (X-Axis)

Shows the time period for each data point. Zoom in/out to see different perspectives.

### Volume

**Volume bars** appear below the price chart, showing how many shares traded during each period.

**Why volume matters:**
- Confirms price moves (high volume = conviction)
- Low volume rallies may not be sustainable
- High volume at support/resistance = significant

### Moving Averages

Lines showing average price over a period (e.g., 50-day, 200-day). Covered in detail in Chapter 15.

## Reading Price Action

### Trends

**Uptrend:** Series of higher highs and higher lows
```
        /\
       /  \  /\
      /    \/  \  /
     /          \/
    /
```

**Downtrend:** Series of lower highs and lower lows
```
    \
     \          /\
      \    /\  /  \
       \  /  \/    \
        \/          \
```

**Sideways/Range:** Price bouncing between support and resistance
```
    ─────/\────/\────/\────
        /  \  /  \  /  \
    ───/────\/────\/────\──
```

### Key Price Levels

**Support:** Price level where buying pressure tends to emerge
- Price has bounced off this level before
- Buyers see value at this price

**Resistance:** Price level where selling pressure tends to emerge
- Price has struggled to break above
- Sellers take profits at this level

### Gaps

**Gap up:** Opens higher than previous close
**Gap down:** Opens lower than previous close

**Types:**
- **Breakaway gap:** Starts a new trend; often doesn't fill
- **Continuation gap:** Middle of a move
- **Exhaustion gap:** End of a trend; often fills

## Volume Analysis Basics

### Volume Confirms Price

**Healthy uptrend:**
- Price rising on increasing volume
- Pullbacks occur on decreasing volume

**Healthy downtrend:**
- Price falling on increasing volume
- Bounces occur on decreasing volume

### Volume at Key Levels

**High volume at support:** Strong buying interest; support likely to hold
**High volume at resistance:** Strong selling interest; resistance likely to hold
**Breakout on high volume:** More likely to be sustained
**Breakout on low volume:** More likely to fail (false breakout)

## Practical Chart Reading

### Step-by-Step Analysis

1. **Identify the trend** - Is price making higher highs/lows or lower highs/lows?
2. **Note key levels** - Where has price bounced or stalled before?
3. **Check volume** - Does volume confirm price action?
4. **Look for patterns** - Are any chart patterns forming?
5. **Consider timeframe context** - What's the bigger picture?

### Example Analysis

*Stock XYZ Daily Chart:*
- Price in uptrend (higher highs and lows)
- Currently pulling back toward $50 support
- Volume declining on pullback (healthy)
- Previous resistance at $55 now potential support
- Weekly chart also shows uptrend

**Interpretation:** Pullback within uptrend; $50 area may offer buying opportunity if support holds.

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Cherry-Picking Patterns

Seeing patterns everywhere, including where they don't exist. Be objective.

### 2. Ignoring Volume

Price moves without volume confirmation are less reliable.

### 3. Single Timeframe Tunnel Vision

What looks like a breakout on a 5-minute chart may be noise on a daily chart.

### 4. Over-Complicating

A chart with 15 indicators is harder to read than one with 2-3. Simplicity often works better.

### 5. Forcing Trades

Not every chart setup is actionable. No setup = no trade.

## Key Takeaways

- **Candlestick charts are standard** - Learn to read them first
- **Timeframe should match your trading style** - Day traders use minutes; investors use weeks/months
- **Volume confirms price** - Pay attention to volume on moves
- **Trend is your friend** - Identify whether price is trending or ranging
- **Support and resistance matter** - Note key levels where price has reacted
- **Use multiple timeframes** - Get the bigger picture before zooming in
- **Keep it simple** - A clean chart is easier to read than a cluttered one
