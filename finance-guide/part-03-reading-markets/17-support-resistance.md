# Chapter 17: Support, Resistance & Trend Lines

## Overview

Support and resistance are price levels where buying or selling pressure has historically emerged, causing price to pause or reverse. These levels form the foundation of most trading strategies—they tell you where to buy, where to sell, and where to place stops.

---

## Understanding Support

### What Is Support?

Support is a price level where buying interest is strong enough to prevent further decline. Think of it as a "floor" under price.

### Why Support Forms

1. **Value buyers:** Investors see value at this price and buy
2. **Previous buyers:** Those who bought before buy more to average down
3. **Short covering:** Short sellers take profits
4. **Psychological levels:** Round numbers attract interest

### Identifying Support

**Previous lows:**
- Price bounced from this level before
- The more times tested, the stronger

**Previous resistance (role reversal):**
- Old resistance becomes new support
- Sellers who sold at resistance are now gone

**Moving averages:**
- 20, 50, 200 EMAs often act as dynamic support

**Round numbers:**
- $50, $100, $500 attract attention

**Gap fills:**
- Unfilled gaps often act as support

---

## Understanding Resistance

### What Is Resistance?

Resistance is a price level where selling pressure is strong enough to prevent further advance. Think of it as a "ceiling" above price.

### Why Resistance Forms

1. **Profit taking:** Traders sell at target prices
2. **Previous sellers:** Those who sold before sell more if wrong
3. **Trapped buyers:** Those who bought high sell to break even
4. **Overhead supply:** Accumulation of willing sellers

### Identifying Resistance

**Previous highs:**
- Price rejected from this level before
- Multiple tests increase significance

**Previous support (role reversal):**
- Old support becomes new resistance
- "Broken floor becomes ceiling"

**Moving averages:**
- Falling MAs often cap rallies

**Round numbers:**
- Similar to support

**Unfilled gaps:**
- Gap resistance from previous sells

---

## The Role Reversal Principle

One of the most important concepts in technical analysis:

**Broken support becomes resistance.**
**Broken resistance becomes support.**

### Why It Works

When a level breaks:
- Previous buyers at support are now underwater and want out
- Previous sellers at resistance missed their chance and want in
- The same people who created the level now act in the opposite direction

### Example

1. Stock has resistance at $50 (tested 3 times)
2. Stock breaks above $50 on high volume
3. Stock pulls back to $50
4. Previous sellers cover; new buyers step in
5. $50 now acts as support

## Drawing Trend Lines

### Uptrend Line

**Drawing rules:**
1. Connect at least two swing lows
2. More touches = stronger line
3. Don't force it—if it doesn't fit naturally, don't draw it

**Using uptrend lines:**
- Buy on touches of the line (support)
- Line break suggests trend change
- Often coincides with moving averages

### Downtrend Line

**Drawing rules:**
1. Connect at least two swing highs
2. More touches = stronger line
3. Adjust as new highs form

**Using downtrend lines:**
- Sell/short on touches of the line (resistance)
- Line break suggests potential reversal
- Watch for failed breakouts

### Trend Line Rules

- **Steeper isn't always better:** Very steep lines break easily
- **Adjust as needed:** Redraw if price action changes
- **Breaks don't mean reversal:** Sometimes just trend deceleration
- **Use closing prices:** Wicks can temporarily break lines

## Horizontal Levels

### Key Characteristics

- **Price clusters:** Where price has spent time
- **High-volume areas:** Where lots of trading occurred
- **Recent highs/lows:** Fresh in traders' minds
- **Historical extremes:** Major highs and lows

### Strength of Levels

**Stronger levels:**
- Multiple tests
- Multiple timeframe alignment
- Recent tests
- High volume at level

**Weaker levels:**
- Single test
- Only visible on one timeframe
- Very old levels
- Low volume at level

### Drawing Horizontal Levels

1. Zoom out to see the bigger picture
2. Identify obvious swing highs and lows
3. Look for price clusters (consolidation areas)
4. Mark levels where price has reacted multiple times
5. Note: Levels are zones, not exact prices

## Channels

### Rising Channel

Two parallel uptrend lines containing price:
- Support line (lower)
- Resistance line (upper)

**Trading:**
- Buy at lower line
- Sell at upper line
- Break below = potential reversal

### Falling Channel

Two parallel downtrend lines:
- Resistance line (upper)
- Support line (lower)

**Trading:**
- Short at upper line
- Cover at lower line
- Break above = potential reversal

### Horizontal Channel (Range)

Price bouncing between flat support and resistance.

**Trading:**
- Buy at support
- Sell at resistance
- Trade breakouts when they occur

## Breakouts and Breakdowns

### Breakout (Above Resistance)

**Valid breakout characteristics:**
- Volume surge (1.5x+ average)
- Strong close near highs
- Follow-through next day
- Retest holds as new support

**False breakout warning signs:**
- Low volume
- Closes well off highs
- Quick reversal back below
- No follow-through

### Breakdown (Below Support)

**Same principles apply:**
- Volume confirmation needed
- Watch for failed breakdowns (bull traps become bearish traps)
- Retest of support (now resistance) is common

### Trading Breakouts

**Aggressive entry:**
- Enter on breakout candle
- Stop below resistance (new support)
- Risk: False breakouts

**Conservative entry:**
- Wait for retest of level
- Enter on confirmation
- Risk: Miss some moves that don't retest

## Multiple Timeframe Analysis

### The Concept

Support/resistance on higher timeframes is more significant than lower timeframes.

**Monthly > Weekly > Daily > Hourly > Minute**

### Practical Application

1. Identify key levels on weekly/monthly chart
2. Zoom to daily for additional levels
3. Use hourly/4-hour for entry timing

### Confluence

When levels align across timeframes, they're more powerful:
- Daily support at $50
- Weekly trendline at $50
- 200-day MA at $50
- **Triple confluence = high-probability level**

## Trading Support and Resistance

### Buying at Support

**Setup:**
1. Identify strong support level
2. Wait for price to reach level
3. Look for reversal candlestick pattern
4. Enter with stop below support
5. Target previous resistance

**Risk management:**
- Stop 1-3% below support level
- Accept some false signals
- Don't average down if support breaks

### Selling at Resistance

**Setup:**
1. Identify strong resistance level
2. Wait for price to reach level
3. Look for reversal pattern
4. Enter short with stop above resistance
5. Target previous support

### Breakout Trading

**Setup:**
1. Identify established range with clear resistance
2. Wait for breakout with volume
3. Enter on breakout or retest
4. Stop below breakout level
5. Target measured move (range height projected upward)

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Exact Prices vs. Zones

Support at $50 doesn't mean it will bounce exactly at $50.00. Think in zones ($49.50-$50.50).

### 2. Drawing Too Many Lines

A chart with 20 lines is useless. Focus on the most significant levels.

### 3. Ignoring the Trend

Buying support in a downtrend works until it doesn't. Trend takes precedence.

### 4. Fighting Breaks

When a major level breaks with volume, don't fight it. Respect the break.

### 5. Not Adjusting

Old levels lose relevance. Update your analysis regularly.

## Support/Resistance Checklist

**For any level you're watching:**

- [ ] Multiple tests?
- [ ] Visible on higher timeframe?
- [ ] Round number?
- [ ] Confluence with moving average?
- [ ] High volume at level?
- [ ] Recent or historical?
- [ ] Where are stops likely placed?

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Support is a floor; resistance is a ceiling** - Where buying/selling pressure historically emerges
- **Role reversal is key** - Broken support becomes resistance (and vice versa)
- **Levels are zones, not exact prices** - Allow for some wiggle room
- **Multiple touches increase strength** - The more tests, the more significant
- **Higher timeframe levels matter more** - Weekly support > daily support
- **Confluence is powerful** - Multiple reasons to watch a level = higher probability
- **Breakouts need volume** - Low-volume breaks often fail
- **The trend matters** - Don't buy support in strong downtrends
