# Personal Finance Guide for Your Early 20s
*Building Wealth Through Smart Saving and Investing*

## Page 1: Why Start Now?

### The Power of Time
- Starting in your 20s gives you 40+ years until retirement
- Time is your greatest asset in wealth building
- Small amounts saved early can outperform larger amounts saved later

### Key Principle: Pay Yourself First
- Treat savings like a bill you must pay
- Automate contributions to remove temptation
- Focus on building habits, not perfection

## Page 2: Understanding Compound Interest

### What Is Compound Interest?
**"Interest earning interest"** - Your money grows exponentially over time

### Real Example
- $500/month invested at 8% annual return:
  - After 10 years: **$91,473**
  - After 20 years: **$274,571**
  - After 30 years: **$679,700**
  - After 40 years: **$1,553,340**

### The Rule of 72
- Divide 72 by your return rate to find doubling time
- 8% return = doubles every 9 years
- 10% return = doubles every 7.2 years

## Page 3: 401(k) - Your Employer Plan

### What Is It?
- Employer-sponsored retirement account
- Pre-tax contributions (Traditional) or after-tax (Roth)
- Money grows tax-deferred

### 2024-2025 Contribution Limits
- **Employee contribution: $23,000/year** ($23,500 in 2025)
- Ages 50+: Additional $7,500 catch-up
- Total with employer match: Up to $69,000

### Key Benefits
- **Employer match = FREE MONEY** (contribute enough to get full match)
- Reduces taxable income (Traditional 401k)
- Automatic payroll deduction

### Common Match Structures
- 50% on first 6% of salary (most common)
- 100% on first 3% of salary
- Dollar-for-dollar up to 4%

---

## Page 4: Traditional vs. Roth 401(k)

### Traditional 401(k)
**Pay taxes later**
- Contributions reduce taxable income NOW
- Money grows tax-deferred
- Pay ordinary income tax on withdrawals in retirement

**Best for:** Higher earners expecting lower tax bracket in retirement

### Roth 401(k)
**Pay taxes now**
- Contributions made with after-tax dollars
- Money grows tax-free
- NO taxes on qualified withdrawals in retirement

**Best for:** Younger workers in lower tax brackets, expecting higher income later

### Pro Tip
You can split contributions between both types!

## Page 5: Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs)

### Traditional IRA
- **2024-2025 Contribution Limit: $7,000/year** ($7,500 in 2025)
- Tax-deductible contributions (income limits apply)
- Tax-deferred growth
- Taxed on withdrawal in retirement
- Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) start at age 73

### Roth IRA
- **2024-2025 Contribution Limit: $7,000/year** ($7,500 in 2025)
- After-tax contributions (not tax-deductible)
- Tax-free growth and withdrawals
- NO RMDs during your lifetime
- Can withdraw contributions anytime penalty-free

### Income Limits (2024)
**Roth IRA phase-out ranges:**
- Single: $146,000 - $161,000
- Married filing jointly: $230,000 - $240,000

## Page 6: Roth IRA - The Young Person's Superpower

### Why Roth IRAs Are Perfect for Your 20s

**1. Flexibility**
- Withdraw contributions anytime, no penalty
- Acts as emergency fund backup

**2. Tax-Free Growth for Decades**
- 40+ years of tax-free compound growth
- Withdraw tax-free in retirement

**3. No RMDs**
- Money can grow your entire lifetime
- Better for estate planning

**4. Backdoor Roth Strategy**
- If income too high: contribute to Traditional IRA, then convert to Roth
- No income limits on conversions

### First-Time Home Purchase Exception
- Can withdraw up to $10,000 of earnings penalty-free

## Page 7: HSA - The Triple Tax Advantage

### Health Savings Account (HSA)
**The most tax-advantaged account available**

### Three Tax Benefits
1. **Tax-deductible** contributions
2. **Tax-free** growth
3. **Tax-free** withdrawals for qualified medical expenses

### 2024-2025 Contribution Limits
- Individual: **$4,150** ($4,300 in 2025)
- Family: **$8,300** ($8,550 in 2025)
- Age 55+: Additional $1,000 catch-up

### Requirements
- Must have High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)
- 2024 HDHP minimum deductible: $1,600 individual / $3,200 family

### Secret Strategy: HSA as Retirement Account
- Pay medical expenses out-of-pocket
- Keep receipts indefinitely
- Invest HSA funds
- Reimburse yourself decades later (no time limit!)
- After age 65: Can withdraw for any reason (taxed like Traditional IRA)

## Page 8: Investment Fund Basics

### Expense Ratios
**Annual fee to manage your investment** (as percentage of assets)

### What's Good?
- **Index funds: 0.03% - 0.20%** ✓
- **Target-date funds: 0.10% - 0.50%**
- **Active funds: 0.50% - 1.50%** ✗

### Impact of Expense Ratios
$100,000 invested over 30 years at 7% return:
- **0.05% fee:** $743,485
- **0.50% fee:** $663,367 (lose $80,118)
- **1.00% fee:** $591,550 (lose $151,935)

### What to Look For
- **Index funds** tracking S&P 500, Total Stock Market
- Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab have excellent low-cost options
- Target-date funds for "set it and forget it"

## Page 9: Common Investment Options

### Stock Index Funds
- Track entire market or segments (S&P 500, Total Market)
- Low fees (0.03% - 0.15%)
- Diversified across hundreds/thousands of companies
- Historical average: ~10% annual return (long-term)

### Target-Date Funds
- "Set it and forget it" option
- Automatically rebalances based on retirement year
- Example: "Target 2060 Fund" for someone retiring around 2060
- Becomes more conservative as target date approaches

### Bond Funds
- Lower risk, lower return than stocks
- Provide stability and income
- Typically 1-5% annual returns
- Increase allocation as you age

### Recommended Starting Allocation (Age 20-30)
- 90% Stock Index Funds
- 10% Bond Funds

## Page 10: Employee Stock Purchase Program (ESPP)

### What Is It?
Employer program allowing you to buy company stock at a discount

### Typical Structure
- Contribute 1-15% of salary through payroll deduction
- **15% discount** on stock price (most common)
- Purchase periods (usually 6 months)
- Some have "lookback provision"

### Lookback Provision Example
- Stock price at start: $100
- Stock price at end: $120
- You pay: $85 (15% off the lower price of $100)
- Instant gain: $35 per share = **41% return!**

### Tax Treatment
- **Ordinary income:** Discount amount
- **Capital gains:** Growth after purchase

### Strategy
- Contribute maximum if possible (free 15%+ return)
- Consider selling immediately to lock in gains
- Avoid concentration risk (don't hold too much company stock)

## Page 11: Optimal Savings Priority Order

### Follow This Order for Maximum Benefit

**1. Emergency Fund First**
- Save $1,000-$2,000 in savings account
- Then build to 3-6 months of expenses

**2. 401(k) to Employer Match**
- Always get the full match (it's free money!)
- If 50% match on 6%, contribute at least 6%

**3. Pay Off High-Interest Debt**
- Credit cards (>15% APR)
- High-interest personal loans

**4. Max Out HSA**
- If you have a HDHP, this is a retirement powerhouse

**5. Max Out Roth IRA**
- $7,000/year contribution

**6. Max Out 401(k)**
- Up to $23,000/year

**7. Taxable Brokerage Account**
- After maxing tax-advantaged accounts

## Page 12: Additional Money-Saving Strategies

### Mega Backdoor Roth
- Some 401(k) plans allow after-tax contributions
- Can contribute up to $46,000 additional (2024)
- Convert to Roth immediately for tax-free growth
- Check if your plan allows this

### 529 Plans (Education Savings)
- Tax-free growth for education expenses
- State tax deductions in many states
- Can change beneficiaries
- Leftover funds can roll to Roth IRA (new rule, up to $35k lifetime)

### Taxable Brokerage Accounts
- No contribution limits
- No early withdrawal penalties
- Long-term capital gains taxed at lower rates (0%, 15%, or 20%)
- Good for goals before retirement

### I Bonds (Savings Bonds)
- Inflation-protected
- $10,000/year limit
- Good for emergency fund or short-term savings
- Must hold at least 1 year

## Page 13: Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Not Starting Early Enough
- Every year you wait costs thousands in compound growth
- Start with whatever amount you can

### 2. Leaving Free Money on Table
- Not contributing enough to get full employer match
- Missing out on ESPP discounts

### 3. High Expense Ratios
- Paying 1% when you could pay 0.05%
- Check your fund fees annually

### 4. Cashing Out 401(k) When Changing Jobs
- 10% early withdrawal penalty + taxes
- Always roll over to IRA or new employer's 401(k)

### 5. Too Conservative Too Young
- Being 100% in bonds or savings accounts in your 20s
- You have time to weather market volatility

### 6. Trying to Time the Market
- Time IN the market beats timing the market
- Stay consistent through ups and downs

## Page 18: Tax Strategies to Remember

### Tax-Advantaged Account Summary

| Account | Contribution | Growth | Withdrawal |
|---------|-------------|---------|------------|
| Traditional 401(k)/IRA | Tax-deductible | Tax-deferred | Taxed as income |
| Roth 401(k)/IRA | After-tax | Tax-free | Tax-free |
| HSA | Tax-deductible | Tax-free | Tax-free (medical) |
| 529 | After-tax | Tax-free | Tax-free (education) |
| Taxable Brokerage | After-tax | Taxed annually | Capital gains tax |

### Tax Bracket Considerations
- **Lower bracket now?** → Choose Roth
- **Higher bracket now?** → Choose Traditional
- **Uncertain?** → Split contributions

### Harvest Tax Losses
- In taxable accounts, sell losing investments to offset gains
- Can deduct up to $3,000/year against ordinary income

## Page 19: Important Terms to Know

### Vesting
- Time required before employer contributions are yours to keep
- Common schedules: Immediate, 3-year cliff, 5-year graded

### Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)
- Must start withdrawing from Traditional 401(k)/IRA at age 73
- Roth IRAs have no RMDs during your lifetime

### Capital Gains
- **Short-term:** Held < 1 year, taxed as ordinary income
- **Long-term:** Held > 1 year, taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20%

### Rebalancing
- Periodically adjusting portfolio back to target allocation
- Typically done annually or when allocations drift >5%

### Dollar-Cost Averaging
- Investing fixed amounts regularly regardless of market conditions
- Reduces impact of market volatility
